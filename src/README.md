# Architecture and Development

## Platform

- **Stack:** Phaser 4, TypeScript and Vite.
- **Hosting:** Cloudflare Workers static assets, no Worker script (`wrangler.jsonc`).
- **Targets:** landscape phone browsers with on-screen touch controls, and desktop browsers with keyboard and mouse controls.
- **Tooling:** Node 24.22.2, Vitest, ESLint with the shared house rules, Prettier, markdownlint, Dependabot, and the Test and AI attributions workflows.

## Site

Vite builds the HTML inputs in [`pages/`](../pages/) and serves the game at `/play/`, the home page at `/`, the gallery at `/art/` and tuning at `/tuning/`. [`vite.config.ts`](../vite.config.ts) uses game-kit's `gameViteConfig` (`@andornaut/game-kit/web/build/vite/gameConfig`); native package exports resolve the pinned shared libraries without source aliases. Build and deployment commands are in the [root README](../README.md).

## Architecture

Player-facing rules live in the [gameplay design](../gameplay/design.md); this section describes their implementation.

### Data-driven entities

- **Entity mechanisms:** code defines mechanisms; typed data selects entity profiles, traits and tuning without dispatching on entity ids. Existing motion profiles map `ease` to the kit's `sineInOut` easing and other travelling profiles to `linear`; hop lift, phase and lazy seeded leg generation remain local. Reusable pure mechanisms belong in the shared kit when both games have compatible consumers. Changes preserve seeded randomness and replay.
- **Behaviour patterns:** `src/data/behaviours.ts` composes reusable state graphs for animals and moving props. A character selects a plan and an instance can replace it with a complete plan. Data names states, animation tags, move or wait primitives, entry heading capture, an optional move or hop distance from the entry point (where `boundary` also holds) and ordered transition conditions; the executor handles either axis without character-specific sequencing. The antagonist and park boat use the same executor. Spatial values resolve from tiles to pixels when the zone loads, while durations remain seconds. Collision and interaction stay separate. Scene-associated patterns await their physical zone introduction; already-seen introductions activate them directly. Attempts reset movement and pattern state. Acquired actors with `idleWatchdog` retain displacement history across graph and escape changes, and attempt bounded, verified supported recovery only in eligible phases (`src/rules/behaviourIdle.ts`). Active tell phases draw a steady 2px red outline with a 1px outer ink border around the current animated frame’s alpha silhouette ([renderer](scenes/bossWarning.ts), [geometry](presentation/bossWarning.ts)), using `BOSS_WARNING` without changing body pixels or contact bounds. Chase, committed attacks and cooldown have no outline. Pursuing bosses acquire when their horizontal centres share an authored chunk or both body centres are in the current camera view. Line of sight and ground reach do not gate acquisition. Settled rearming uses the same awareness with its existing reach guards. Attack-specific `visible` checks still require both body centres inside the current camera view and a clear line through solid terrain and solid objects; ledges do not occlude. `src/rules/perception.ts` supplies the shared chunk and sight predicates.
- **Behaviour pipeline:**
  - **Tick:** each tick runs perceive, decide and act for every behaviour-driven object.
  - **Perceive:** `src/rules/zone.ts` builds one [`BehaviourPerception`](rules/behaviourPerception.ts) per object, holding Chibi's state and tuning and the behaviour world: present terrain, platforms, hazards and chunks, the camera view, the traversal frame with support and crumbling predictions, the targeted-attack window and an object-local `RandomStreams` factory named from the zone seed, attempt, area and object index.
  - **Decide:** for each slice of the step, `decideSlice` in [`behaviour.ts`](rules/behaviour.ts) evaluates support ([`behaviourSupport.ts`](rules/behaviourSupport.ts)), escape settling, entry effects, the first matching unscored transition, the engaged state's scored decision and how long the decision holds, from the snapshot and the object's own layout and state only. [`behaviourAttack.ts`](rules/behaviourAttack.ts) keeps the attack phase, the post-completion cooldown and the plan's `tellCancelPause`: after that many consecutive cancelled tells no tell starts for its seconds while pursuit continues, and a commit, the pause or an escape resets the count. A continuing tell (`attack.continues`) re-tells inside its committed attack and either commits again or completes it.
  - **Scored decisions:** a boss plan's `scorer` (`BossScorer`, values in [`bossScorers.ts`](data/bossScorers.ts)) runs in its engaged state (`pursuit`). That state's attacks are transitions carrying a `score`: their `when` is a hard gate (with `attack-ready` on every tell) and game-kit's `ai/utility` `scoreActions` chooses among the gated attacks and the scorer's moves, never transition order. [`behaviourScorer.ts`](rules/behaviourScorer.ts) builds the candidates: considerations read distance, horizontal gap, height gap and delta, line of sight, Chibi's speed, and the engaged seconds outside attacks since the last chosen attack (`threatSeconds`) and special (`specialDueSeconds`); pressure scales attacks and closing moves up, and a standoff down, after a grace, and cadence scales an attack up as its 20 s or 40 s cadence comes due. Moves (`chase`, `cut-off` ahead of his velocity, `ahead` along his travel, `standoff` on the boss's side) are decided at a seeded 0.6 to 0.9 s interval when a grounded or safely hovering boss can retarget; the chosen move holds its target rect (`BehaviourState.intent`), which ground chase and map pursuit, air chase and overhead flight aim at. A gated attack is scored as soon as its gate holds, against the held move. Each decision draws from `world.decisions("scorer:<n>")`. Tells, committed attacks, airborne legs and takeoff preparation never decide. The map's pace is the fallback when no path reaches the move's target.
  - **Chase relief:** a plan's `pursuit.relief` (`ChaseRelief`) pauses the engaged state after a seeded span of chase outside attacks, drawn with the pause's length from `world.decisions("relief:<n>")`. The pause holds the boss facing Chibi in the idle pose (Pebble in a safe hover), starts only with a map path, on the ground and the tell clearance after a tell or committed attack, and holds tells for the clearance after it ends; its end restarts the scorer's `pressureSeconds`. Each engagement draws its give-up time in `giveUp.seconds` from `world.decisions("give-up:<n>")`; the first pause starting at or after it gives up, as does a pause still running when it passes, and after `unreachableGraceSeconds` without a map path the boss gives up. A give-up ends pursuit like the escape distance, with the escape bark and escape event. After any ended chase (give-up, distance or door), a settled ground boss walks at chase speed toward its encounter position, stopping at walls and support edges, and `pursuit.rearmSeconds` pass before awareness engages again.
  - **Wander:** a plan's `wander` (`BossWander`) moves the boss in a wander wait (`motion.wander`, the initial state) while it is outside any ended chase's settling, walk home and rearm cooldown (`wanderStep` in [`behaviour.ts`](rules/behaviour.ts)). Each move draws its interval, hold, side and distance from `world.decisions("wander:<n>")`. A ground boss walks the distance in the engaged chase's pose at the walk speed toward the drawn side, or the other side when that one leaves `range` around its encounter position or gives less than half the shortest walk, stopping at walls and support edges; a flyer enters its `hop` state, a supported flight with a `home` leash that returns to the wander state on landing. The hold after a move lasts the drawn hold, or the rest of the drawn interval when longer, facing the move's direction in the wander state's pose.
  - **Act:** `actSlice` maps the decided motion to an ability kind and acts it through the explicit [ability table](rules/behaviourAbilities.ts): wait and supported dwell, track travel, target flight, supported landing, air chase, overhead swoop, ground chase, escape settling ([pursuit abilities](rules/behaviourPursuitAbilities.ts)) and ground fall, which drops a ground boss whose support has gone before its committed motion acts. A ground boss's committed horizontal move stops at the edge of its connected support. After an escape, every ground boss move and wait also falls once its support has gone and stops at that edge, and the edge or wall that stops a wander walk ends it. Current support is ordinary contact: feet on a support top under part of the hitbox; chosen landing destinations put the whole frame on one support. Abilities return the slice end, next state, carried support and traversal legs; graph checks reach them only through decision callbacks.
  - **Movement maps:** [`movementMaps.ts`](rules/movementMaps.ts) builds game-kit movement maps (ground walkers, jumpers and Kiwi's climb links, with fixed-impulse jump scripts) and air maps (Pebble, with perches from the supported-landing rule) per pursuer profile per area. Profiles come from each boss's pursuit data; play reads only the cached pursuit maps (Map pursuit). Static terrain and fixed platforms are colliders; static hazards block. Moving platforms are left out, and crumbling and clocked supports, timed hazards and moving hazards are node and edge deps that a `MapMask` disables per query; climb edges are masked out of pursuit routes. Witnesses run through game-kit's default kernel step, which samples its own collider grid; hazard, blocker and dependency queries read a uniform grid over their rects. [`pathOut.ts`](sim/pathOut.ts) checks a path out of an encounter: every boss spawn and reachable spot reaches an exit and the spawn, every Chibi refuge has a skill-free path out, and every map edge on a rearm route replays and proves through pursuit's kernel. Certification runs it on every encounter candidate and arena; [`movementMap.test.ts`](sim/movementMap.test.ts) runs it on Archie's sample arena. `node src/sim/mapBench.ts` reports build steps and wall time per profile per stage area (check container only); `--window leash` or `--window <tiles>` clips each build around the boss spawn.
  - **Map pursuit:** each ground pursuer's route map (`pursuitGroundMap`: its pursuit physics without climbing, clipped to its leash's columns) is built for every area when the zone starts (`preparePursuitMaps` in `startZone`, behind stage loading) and cached per area, profile and window; play steps only read the cache, and the zone hands each pursuer's map to perception as `groundMap`. [`behaviourMapPursuit.ts`](rules/behaviourMapPursuit.ts) locates the boss, takes Chibi's nodes as the boss-body spans on his support that meet his body, and masks climb and track-climb edges, edges whose leg proof failed, and nodes and edges whose crumbling support is absent or of unknown future presence or whose sweep a timed or moving hazard may meet. While engaged, ground chase takes the first edge of the fastest path: it walks to the edge's launch point and runs the witness through `advanceTraversalLeg`, which proves it with the edge's commands as preferred inputs (split at a part-way landing, the rest continuing as the next leg) under the usual takeoff preparation and re-proof. The chosen route is kept until the boss's node, Chibi's support, the mask or his position to within four tiles changes. A chase on Chibi's node that makes no progress (a mover the map leaves out) paces back from the blocked spot. A failed proof disables the edge until the boss's node, Chibi's support or the crumbling key changes or a pace turns. With no path the boss walks to `nearestReachable` toward Chibi and paces there (`pursuit.pace`) at the pace speed, holding a seeded pause facing Chibi (`world.decisions("pace:<n>")`) when the pace starts and at each turn, and a mapped boss never matches `lost-target` while engaged. Rearming, engaged reachability and ground idle-recovery goals read the same map. Off the map (airborne, on a mover, or Chibi on a mover) pursuit uses the on-demand planner. A takeoff within `prepIntervalSeconds` of the last preparation's start launches without one. `node src/sim/mapBench.ts --decisions` times the runtime builds and, over every node pair, a decision that chooses a new route and one that keeps it.
  - **Recovery and effects:** idle recovery is the entry in a [recovery table](rules/behaviourRecoveryAbilities.ts), and the zone turns entry actions into force pulses and spawns through an [effect table](rules/behaviourEffects.ts). `stepBehaviour` perceives and advances in one call for fixtures.
  - **Game-independent core:** game-independent mechanisms live in `src/ai/` without game imports: the ability interface, result and table, seeded stream factories and agent traces. Lint rejects `Math.random`, `Date` and `performance` under `src/ai/` and `src/rules/`.
  - **Trace fixtures:** the `src/sim/behaviourTrace*.test.ts` suites hash a per-tick FNV-1a 64 trace of every behaviour-driven object, its transients and Chibi over every chunk's required walk, planned stage runs and the boss regression chunks against `src/sim/fixtures/behaviourTraces/`. Behaviour-level traces of every boss graph state and zone-level entry-effect traces cover attacks the zone scenarios do not reach (`src/rules/fixtures/`). `BEHAVIOUR_TRACE_DUMP=<dir>` writes full zone traces to diff against a dump from the base commit; `BEHAVIOUR_TRACE_UPDATE=1` re-records hashes for an intended behaviour change.
  - **Boss defeat:** a boss on a red hazard or below the level dies ([boss defeat](rules/bossDefeat.ts)): its behaviour holds while `BOSS_REMOVAL_SECONDS` of removal play, it loses collision and its pursuit, and the zone keeps it dead through area links within the attempt; a retry restores it. The scene shows the removal through [`bossRemovalView`](presentation/bossRemoval.ts): the `boss-removal` sprite's `puff` tag when that texture is loaded, holding its first frame with reduced motion, otherwise a fade.

- **Boss simulator:** [`stepZoneBodies`](rules/zone.ts) is the physical part of the zone step: object frames with every behaviour object's perceive, decide and act, crumbling, forced transit, Chibi's body, contact and death; `stepZone` adds barks, doors, walk links and the stage exit. Both take the bird tuning under each skill selection ([`SKILL_PROFILES`](progress/skills.ts)) as their first parameter, which decides ordinary forced-transit control. The game-independent [`simulate`](ai/simulation.ts) driver steps a model at a fixed clock with an injected controller and records a structured [agent trace](ai/agentTrace.ts) and its FNV-1a 64 hash. [`bossSimulator.ts`](sim/bossSimulator.ts) adapts it to bosses: it runs only `stepZoneBodies`, retries with every behaviour active after a death, ends when Chibi leaves the area and records each boss's state, motion, position, footing and support, pursuit phase, attack phase and cooldown, Chibi and the targeted-attack window per tick. Chibi is scripted or the seeded reactive bot, and a scenario may place him at given ticks, applied at the end of the previous step so its sample records where the bosses next perceive him. [Boss scenarios](testing/bossScenarios.ts) cover past regressions and each boss, on the admitted arenas, the sample arenas and synthetic placements on surviving chunks; [`bossSimulator.test.ts`](sim/bossSimulator.test.ts) proves the boss trace equals `stepZone`'s on every scenario and reproduces the recorded encounter trace hashes. [Boss properties](sim/bossProperties.ts) check the gameplay boss rules with thresholds read from behaviour data, through the generic [property checks](ai/agentProperties.ts); Scenario groups can add their own checks, such as the rearm crossing after a give-up and return. `bossProperties.test.ts` keeps the current behaviour's failures in an explicit known-failure list. Fairness bounds per boss (`BossFairness`, values in [`bossFairness.ts`](data/bossFairness.ts)) are read only by checks: a reaction floor from tell start to contact, the threat gap and the recovery bound as boss properties, and dodge witnesses: [`bossDodges.ts`](testing/bossDodges.ts) forks the zone at every tell start in every boss scenario and runs the attack's named [dodge scripts](testing/dodges.ts) after the reaction delay, and `bossDodges.test.ts` requires one to keep Chibi alive through the attack and its recovery.
- **Traversal:** [behaviour traversal](rules/behaviourTraversal.ts) adapts actual current-frame terrain, moving platforms and hazards to the native game-kit planner. A ground pursuit walks at its `speed` on a grounded step without planned inputs; searched and planned traversal (takeoffs, air, map legs, proofs and movement maps) moves at its `traversal.speed`, and a pace walks both at `pace.speed`. Unsupported or incomplete future geometry remains unknown. Preferred command prefixes share the original query’s work limits and terminal safety checks. Without a movement map (fixtures and predictions), a settled boss's rearm proof is one direct search; an exhausted proof is unknown, not unreachable. Boss anticipation uses the actual preparation clock, keeping animation and silhouette contact on the same pose.

### Stage lifecycle

- **Opening:** `OpeningScene` plays the ordered beats in `src/data/opening.ts`, deriving motion from art's normalized recipe. Shot durations animate poses and transforms; each beat waits for fresh Continue, and Skip exits the entire sequence. New Game and confirmed Restart open it; saved Continue opens the map. Only completion or Skip acknowledges the dedicated progress marker, so interruption restarts at the first beat. Opening time does not start stage statistics.
- **Dialogue:**
  - **Introductions:** an unseen destination introduction opens at entry to the stage ID selected by `ZoneDialoguePlan.introStage`, or its first stage when omitted. Selectors outside the destination are rejected; earlier stages leave the introduction unseen. `ZoneIntroductionScene` draws its data-selected antagonist outside the physical object list, holds the world while the actor enters from beyond the current viewport, reaches its horizontal centre and speaks through a manual conversation. When the actor turns and starts running away, `introductionDepartureStarted` releases only the introduction-owned freeze and input scope; other pause or modal owners retain their hold. The separate, noncolliding actor continues offscreen before removal. Its entry profile selects the character, animation tags and shared approach/departure speed derived from the maximum charge speed; cinematic art has no contact body, projectiles or encounter quota. Completing or skipping the conversation remembers the introduction per destination for the playthrough, so retries and replays omit it.
  - **Conversations:** ordinary conversations use `DialogueScene`, with plans and speakers in `src/data/dialogues.ts`, paragraph text in `TEXT.lines`, and names and controls in `TEXT.dialogue`. The fixed top panel shows one paragraph, at most two lines at the shared font size, centred down the panel by ink. Each speaker names a portrait side: the bird remains left and other speakers right; images are repositioned without mirroring. The speaker's name tab rises above the panel's top at the end opposite the portrait. The panel and its name tab form one press area. Secondary Skip and primary Continue share a compact footer below the panel. The name tab's top sits `DIALOGUE_LAYOUT.nameTab.clearance` below the skill HUD's drawn discs ([`src/data/layout.ts`](data/layout.ts)), clear of their targets; the panel and the footer derive from it, so the HUD stays live above every panel. Every dialogue, bark, gift-room helper and achievement notice panel draws its background at `DIALOGUE_PANEL_ALPHA` (`src/data/dialogues.ts`), in the HTML UI's shared panel or, in the gift room, through [`drawDialoguePanel`](scenes/dialoguePanel.ts); portraits, name tabs and text stay opaque. Fresh taps or non-repeating keys other than P and Escape advance; P and Escape pass on to `PauseScene`. Tab and arrows focus actions, and Enter or Space activates a focused action. Held touches and drags do not advance.
  - **Choices:** Skip and the final paragraph open a required terminal choice when the plan defines one, otherwise end the conversation. `DialogueChoiceScene` uses the shared card and buttons, starts without a focused answer and cannot close through Skip, back, Escape or an outside tap. The zone remains held through the choice; only a selected option id is returned, with no actions or flags applied. All dialogue input other than P and Escape is consumed. The dialogue stops before notifying its opener once through `dialogueClosed(selection?)`.
  - **Barks:** a behaviour may declare one `spotted` speaker/line pair: the engine emits it once per placed encounter per attempt, retaining that record across area re-entry and clearing it on retry. `ZoneBarkScene` shows queued notices live for `BARK_DURATION_SECONDS`, without footer controls or a modal input scope. A fresh guarded panel tap dismisses only the current line and preserves queued lines. Notices remain with the world below modal backdrops, pause with it and clear on retry or shutdown. A notice with an owner starts and stays only while that actor is on screen; [stage bark schedules](rules/stageBarks.ts) offer Fert's journey lines without an owner, as his offscreen voice.
- **Death:** a failed attempt freezes the world and first pans the camera to frame the final presentation floor at the viewport bottom and the sprite frame horizontally at its centre. The pan uses data-configured speed and duration limits with sine easing; an already aligned camera adds no wait. The bird then recoils, falls onto resolved frozen support, squashes on impact and makes a small upward bounce into a seated rest. Void falls retain their configured visible fall above the final floor. Actual lethal object and transient checks record contact provenance; only author-marked red sources trigger `src/scenes/contactFeedback.ts`. Its data-configured camera jolt uses captured world-edge pixels for render-only overscan and restores camera state before the card, retry or shutdown. Controls stay fixed, and reduced motion retains a static border cue without camera movement. `src/scenes/deathCamera.ts` completes the camera pan before animation; the same framing remains through the single death sequence and card capture, with no replay or card-stage relocation. Temporary contact feedback is cancelled before camera scroll and bounds are restored on retry, exit or shutdown, with actors and simulation state preserved. The required `DeathScene` card stays centred horizontally and vertically and holds indefinitely, showing the zone and numbered stage names, current-attempt progress, Last time, Best time and Attempts alongside Stage total, Zone total and Run total. Outside taps, back, Escape and P cannot close the card. It is an HTML `alertdialog` in the UI layer (`src/ui/dom/death.ts`), named by `TEXT.death.label` and described by its stage, progress and statement lines, with the metrics as two description lists in the shared run statistics (`src/ui/dom/runStats.ts`), equal columns either side of the ink divider. Focus starts on the card, where a fresh Enter or Space presses Try again; Tab and the arrows step through Try again then Back to title, which shows on the left. Held input is cleared across transitions, and pause controls stay inert. A four-pixel stage-progress strip at the canvas bottom uses an ink border, a dark-slate track and yellow/amber fill rows; it tracks the current attempt, including backward movement. Death presentation and modal time do not add to active stage time.
- **Stage completion:** [catalogue routing](progress/stageCompletion.ts) saves a nonfinal clear and opens its actual stage preview with Continue, which enters the next stage directly. A destination’s final stage saves the clear, then shows its outgoing exchange (`ZoneDialoguePlan.outro`) in `DialogueScene` over the cleared stage’s actual preview, with Continue at the bottom right and Skip beside it. Completing or skipping it opens the statistics in `ZoneDepartureScene`, whose Continue starts map travel; the final catalogue stage continues to its celebration instead. The preview-first summary has two unboxed three-row columns: Last time, Best time and Attempts on the left; Stage total, Zone total and Run total on the right, with one antagonist statement. [Projection](scenes/CutsceneScene.ts) (`stageSummaryWords`) reads cumulative stage/zone time separately from attempt-local values. Run total displays active time only; deaths and completion bookkeeping remain internal. Back, outside taps and held input do not bypass the interstitial, and presentation time does not count as play.

### Screens and UI

- **Scenes:** `BootScene` runs first: it loads everything `public/art/manifest.json` lists and makes an animation of each sprite tag, keyed `<sprite>:<tag>`, with the tag's frames and durations from the sprite's json-hash sheet. A tag the sprite's manifest entry lists in `once` plays once and holds its last frame; every other tag loops.
- **Map screen:**
  - **Overview:** six themed destinations each group an ordered catalogue of stage-node ids. The overview shows every regional miniature and name; locked markers use padlocks and unbuilt markers use question marks. Unbuilt stages have no playable plan and cannot be entered.
  - **Stage cards:** only manually pressing Continue opens `StageSelectScene`. Its catalogue-ordered cards show actual stage screenshots, standard-size names and smaller stage-number labels. Card capacity derives from the available view width; larger catalogues use pages of full-size cards and open on the latest available stage's page. Hidden cards cannot receive input, and changing pages invalidates held presses. The latest unlocked stage uses the primary style; locked later stages remain visible, grey and disabled. Earlier stages remain replayable. A press on a locked card, or on a page button with no page to turn to, plays the refusal cue (`refuseControl` in `src/ui/dom/refusal.ts`, on the kit's `refusalCue`): the control glides out and back while its labels flash red, and the Reduced motion setting holds them steadily red without moving; a cue already playing is not restarted. Locked cards never activate or enter keyboard focus, and are `aria-disabled` and described as locked. Modal-stack changes, page turns and shutdown stop the cue. Closing, back, Escape and outside taps return to the map without selecting a stage. A new navigation press must focus an answer before keyboard activation, so opening input never selects one.
  - **Progress:** completed stage ids preserve unlocks across replay, and only the configured final apartment stage triggers celebration. The progress format retains catalogue stage ids as additional stages are built.
  - **Controls:** Back to title remains secondary on the left; Continue remains primary on the right and the default map action. Tab selects footer actions, while Left/Right and A/D select unlocked destinations. Returning to the title preserves progress.
  - **Travel:** after a destination's final stage clears, including a replay, the map animates the bird along the drawn route to the next built destination. Input remains locked during travel, the saved position changes on arrival, and arrival does not launch a stage. The final stage retains its celebration.
- **Play view:** the canvas fills `#game`, so where `#game` is wider or taller than 2:1 the canvas is too. Its WebGL backing is in device pixels: r = min(`devicePixelRatio`, `MAX_RENDER_SCALE` (2)) per CSS pixel, set by the page-fit adapter's `deviceResolution` (Phaser's `Scale.NONE` at zoom 1 / r), so `gameSize`, pointers and camera viewports are device pixels. Above r, the browser scales the canvas by `devicePixelRatio` / r with `image-rendering: pixelated`. Layout stays in the 720x360 base pixels: every camera has its origin at the top-left and a zoom of r times the fit (the CSS pixels per base pixel the area takes on both axes, `renderFit` in `@andornaut/game-kit/view/renderScale`), possibly fractional, so `scrollX` and `scrollY` are the view's top-left in base pixels and `src/camera` keeps the world camera in the area without Phaser's bounds. Phaser's `worldView` assumes a zoom about the camera's centre, so the terrain tilemap culls to the view the camera shows from its own origin (`cullToCameraView`, `@andornaut/game-kit/view/phaser/tilemapCull`). Each scene's play camera shows the game's view at the canvas's top, centred across on a whole device pixel (`src/ui/layout/playView.ts`, `src/scenes/playCamera.ts`), and the canvas's own bars, in the page's ink, fill the rest; `canvasRect` is the whole canvas in base pixels. Pointers convert to base pixels before any hit test or tap and drag distance (`playPoint`, and the canvas in base pixels for page touches through `pageToGame`). Textures sample nearest unless classified linear: BootScene sets every manifest texture's filter (`src/scenes/textureFilters.ts`), nearest for all authored pixel art, small UI art included, at the fractional scale, and linear for the antialiased fonts. Line widths stay in base pixels. The UI overlay (`src/scenes/uiPresentation.ts`, the kit's `uiSubmitter` and `uiProjection`) repaints the UI scenes and the zone's UI objects on a 2D canvas over the WebGL canvas at its CSS size times the full `devicePixelRatio`: it captures the WebGL backing's device pixels and projects them by `devicePixelRatio` / r (`src/scenes/uiOverlayFit.ts`), and draws UI text with the TTF faces at the bitmap fonts' metrics. Modal backdrops capture the whole backing and draw it over the canvas in base pixels; contact feedback snapshots the view's device pixels and cuts its edges at the camera's zoom. Every control (pause, the skill HUD, Use, Jump and the move hint) is drawn inside the view, placed in the view's coordinates and moved with the view (`viewShift`), so none moves into a bar (`src/data/layout.ts`): the pause disc and the skill HUD's discs `HUD_EDGE` (12) in from the view's top, the pause disc 12 in from its left and the rightmost skill disc 12 in from its right, so the two top corners mirror, with each skill's 48px target centred on its disc. Jump and the move hint are placed from the canvas's edges instead, which are the page's edges inside the safe-area insets: Jump's drawn ring `CONTROL_SIDE` (60) in from the canvas's right and `CONTROL_BOTTOM` (40) up from its bottom, and the move hint's 60 in from its left, level with Jump, each then held at least `CONTROL_VIEW_MARGIN` (8) inside the view's edge, so none is drawn against a bar (`touchLayout` in `src/ui/layout/touchControls.ts`, from the bars `viewBars` measures). With no bars they sit 60 in from the view's sides and 40 up from its bottom; beside side bars they move out toward the page's edges, and above a band below the view they move down, as far as the margin allows. Use keeps `TOUCH_LAYOUT.useFromJump` from Jump, up and in from it, so it stays inside the view. `#game`'s margins and the page's top padding keep the canvas clear of the device's safe-area insets (`src/main.css`, `public/site.css`), so no control can fall inside one; its left and right margins both take the larger of the left and right insets (`--inset-side`), as does the slim nav column on the left, so a one-sided notch leaves the view centred. The zone draws them in a second camera over the whole canvas, as the pause scene draws the pause button and the dim, and reads a touch's button through the same shift. The move hint (`src/ui/chrome/moveHint.ts`) is a drawing with no press area: at rest it sits level with Jump, as far in from the canvas's left as Jump is from its right, clamped the same way; while a drag moves him, its ring sits where the drag began, held left or right the way he moves, with its knob up to 12 pixels left or right of the ring's centre the way the drag goes, and it returns to rest on release. The Phaser page-fit adapter (`@andornaut/game-kit/web/view/phaser/followFrameTurn`) keeps the page no taller than the visible area (the visual viewport, not viewport units, which an installed app can size past the screen) and refits until the sizes settle on every signal that the visible area or the frame's turn may have changed, with a check four times a second while the page is visible for a fit or turn gone stale with no signal.

- **Components:** each `src/` folder is a component that imports only the components below it, bottom first: `data`; `core`; `ai` and `assets`; `camera` and `stage`; `rules`; `progress`; `presentation` and `ui`; `crash`; `loading`; `pwa`; the side pages (`headers`, `homePage`, `gallery`, `tuningPage`); `scenes`; then the tooling (`dev`, `sim`, `tools`). `CONTEXTS` in [`eslint.config.mjs`](../eslint.config.mjs) lists what each may import, with each remaining upward import as a named module exception, and `import-x/no-cycle` forbids value import cycles; type-only imports are exempt, and so are the files of the one remaining cycle (`CYCLE_EXEMPT_FILES`: `timedHazard` imports `behaviour`, and `behaviourTraversal` and `behaviourMapPursuit` import `timedHazardSafe` from `timedHazard`). Files outside `assets`, `camera`, `core`, `presentation`, `progress`, `stage` and `ui` import them only through their `index.ts` (`INDEXED`, enforced by `import-x/no-restricted-paths`), besides the few modules `INDEXED` names: modules that start page state on load stay out of the indexes, and the loading entry imports only what the loading strip draws.
- **Core:** [`src/core/`](core/) holds the geometry and time primitives every simulation layer shares: the fixed step (`STEP_SECONDS`), the edge tolerance (`EDGE`), `sameRect` and `rectAt`, swept contact, the terrain broadphase (`collisionCandidates`) and sprite silhouette contact.
- **Shared stage geometry:** [`src/stage/`](stage/) holds the tile geometry (`Tileset`, `drawsFrom`, `tileHitbox`) and scenery placement (`worldScenery`, `sceneryParallax`) that rules and presentation both read; `src/presentation/` picks what to draw.
- **Phaser boundary:** only `src/scenes/` and `src/main.ts` import Phaser or a scene, enforced by ESLint's `no-restricted-imports`. Logic, layout and data are Phaser-free, so they unit-test without a renderer.
- **Assets:** [`src/assets/`](assets/) reads art's files as the game uses them: the manifest and json-hash sprite sheets, the build's atlas manifest, sprite contact silhouettes and the bird's frame size (`BIRD_FRAME`).
- **Progress:** [`src/progress/`](progress/) holds progress through the game: the map and its destinations, stage completion routing, the opening, skill ownership and selection, achievement judging, and the saved progress, statistics and earned achievements (`save/`). The page save calls take the page's `SaveSession` first, which reports whether a developer session is running and the developer skill overrides; [`pageSave.ts`](progress/save/pageSave.ts) opens the page's saves on load, so it stays out of the index; [`scenes/save.ts`](scenes/save.ts) binds its session to `src/dev/mode.ts`, and the scenes call the bound functions.
- **UI:** [`src/ui/`](ui/) holds the interface: screen and card layout (`layout/`), which computes every position, size and press area from the layout configuration in [`src/data/layout.ts`](data/layout.ts) (control insets, button, card, panel and HUD geometry), dialogue (`dialogue/`), input intent (`input/`), keyboard focus (`focus/`), pause and room input (`pause/`), touch and motion settings (`settings/`), control chrome (`chrome/`) and the map's drawing (`map/`). [`ui/input/frameTurn.ts`](ui/input/frameTurn.ts) starts the page's frame watcher on load, so it is imported directly and stays out of the index.
- **Presentation:** [`src/presentation/`](presentation/) picks each sprite's tag and whole-pixel position from the game state, Phaser-free, so the scenes only draw what it picks.
- **Website boundary:** the version-pinned game-kit package's `@andornaut/game-kit/web/*` modules provide page orientation, PWA and install flows, back navigation, crash UI, DOM progress, browser save adapters and Vite/header tooling. Atlas codecs and packing come from the version-pinned art-kit package. Only `src/main.ts` imports the kit's Phaser page-fit adapter, which keeps the renderer import boundary. Game policies and application wrappers stay in their existing directories and pass game values to the kit as parameters.
- **Test helpers:** `src/testing/` is importable only from tests. Game tests own rules, authored data, adapter mappings and lifecycle wiring. Shared-library implementations and public contracts belong to their library suites; consumer tests mock those APIs only to verify real game transformations or lifecycle outcomes.

### Assets and build

- **Shared kit:** `@andornaut/game-kit` is installed from the version-pinned GitHub release tarball in `package.json`, with checksum integrity in `package-lock.json`. The installed package's `SOURCE_COMMIT` identifies its canonical revision. Pure game rules, geometry, clocks, save contracts and stores, and explicit Phaser adapters remain in the kit.
- **Atlas:** a production build packs every PNG `public/art/manifest.json` lists (sprites, tilesets and backdrops) whole into atlas pages, each image's edge pixels repeated 1 px around it, written as lossless WebP with every pixel kept ([atlas adapter](tools/atlas/atlasPlugin.ts)) under `/assets/art/` with a content hash in the name (`src/tools/atlas/`); the atlas manifest that maps each art key and frame to its page and rect is built into the game's code. `BootScene` loads the pages and gives each key a texture that shares its page's source (`src/scenes/atlasArt.ts`), so the page uploads to the GPU once, batches draw from one texture, and code that names a key and its frames works as with the key's own files. Fonts load from dedicated PNG/FNT pairs in both modes. Only their isolated texture sources use linear filtering; sprite atlas sources retain nearest filtering (`src/scenes/textureFilters.ts`, which fails to type-check for a manifest texture list with no filter). Standalone manifest images load separately in both modes. The dev server has no atlas and loads each file on its own. Art's files stay as art writes them.
- **Phaser build:** the game runs on WebGL alone, with no sound. A production build bundles Phaser from its source without the namespaces the game does not use, the Canvas renderer or the sound manager (`PHASER_BUILD` in `src/tools/phaserBuild.ts`, built by the kit's Phaser build plugin, `@andornaut/game-kit/web/build/vite/phaserBuildPlugin`; its test fails when the game uses something it leaves out), in its own hashed chunk, so an update that changes only game code keeps Phaser's file. The dev server and Vitest load the package's own bundle.
- **Loading:** the initial HTML and lightweight entry show a separate two-pixel themed loading strip at the top through shared kit geometry, the kit's DOM progress adapter (`@andornaut/game-kit/web/progress/dom/progressBar`) and the kit's Phaser adapter. The strip shows only loads the player waits for, each with its own entry point in `src/loading/progress.ts`. In the foreground (`loadingProgress`): boot (the pre-script `startupLoading`, then BootScene's file load, then `beginStartupChecks`) and entering a zone (`beginStageLoading`). In the background (`backgroundProgress`): the first visit's precache (`beginPrecache`) and a forced update for a newer save (`beginRequiredUpdate`). Background loads during play have no entry point and never show it: `trackOfflineActivity` (`src/loading/serviceWorker.ts`) shows the kit's `"precaching"` offline activity alone, which the kit reports only for the first install on a page no service worker controlled when offline play started, and shows nothing for `"updating"` (every other install) or `"idle"` (including a controlled page's registration). Phaser's loader runs with the kit's `LOADER_CONFIG` (`@andornaut/game-kit/load/loaderConfig`, the game config's `loader`): each file request times out after 30 s and is tried twice more after a timeout or network error, an HTTP error status failing at once, so a load run always completes. `BootScene` is the only scene that loads files; zone entry loads none, since boot loads all the art. Its `preload` starts the kit's loader watch (`watchLoader`, `@andornaut/game-kit/load/phaser/watchLoader`, with `window` as its timer), which applies that timeout to every queued file and ends with the scene, and marks the run as waited (`waitFor("boot")`). The waited run's share is the kit's `createFileShare`: settled files, loaded or failed, out of the run's total, never moving back when the run queues more files, and 1 when it completes. A failed file is logged once with `console.warn` and recorded; a run that completes with recorded files schedules a retry round 15, 60 and then 300 seconds later (`RETRY_ROUNDS_MS`), which queues the failed files again in a run that reports nothing to the strip, and a file that fails its third round is logged as given up. A bitmap font or Aseprite sheet is a multi-file load, recorded once however many of its files fail and queued again whole through the loader method that queued it, so the watch starts before `preload` queues any file. The startup checks after the file load, reading the save and preparing the HTML UI, are a pending phase of their own (`beginStartupChecks`); offline play never holds boot. The strip follows the kit's `createLayeredProgress` over both states, with `LOADING_TIMING`'s hold (`window` as its timer): the foreground shows whenever it is active and the background whenever it alone is. When the shown layer's measured work finishes, the bar holds full for `LOADING_TIMING.fullMs` and fades over `LOADING_TIMING.fadeMs`, and the background shows only once both have passed; a foreground load that begins meanwhile, such as the startup checks, cuts the hold short and shows at once. Unknown work that finishes hands over at once. Startup checks, stage preparation, the precache and the forced update are indeterminate: the kit's travelling segment until they finish. Idle shows nothing; reduced motion does not change it. The motion is the kit's static `progressBar.css`, which the entry imports. The initial HTML is the same bar's pending markup (`src/loading/html.ts`): one `.progress-bar-band` per fill, sized at full by `progressBarLayout`, each holding its segment and clipped by the build's loading stylesheet, since the kit sets the bands' overflow only from script. That stylesheet also sets the bar's timing from `LOADING_TIMING` (cycle, full hold, fade and segment) and its segment width and step count from `progressBarPixelProperties`, as the script-mounted bar does, so the pre-script segment steps its travel one whole pixel at a time. A recreated stage-themed bar is reported the current snapshot at once. Stage entry yields a paint before synchronous assembly, keeps input and updates disabled, and releases the strip after the first prepared frame. The precache does not delay play; its indicator follows the shared offline-activity callback without changing registration timing. While any modal input scope is open, `#game[data-modal-open]` raises the Phaser canvas above the DOM strip so all modal scrims and panels cover it. Progress continues updating underneath; closing the final scope restores the ordinary strip layer, and game destruction removes the layer subscription. Preview capture suppresses loading UI.
- **Crash screen:** the page's first module, `src/loading/entry.ts`, first imports `src/crash/install.ts`, so `src/crash/crashScreen.ts` installs the kit's crash screen (`@andornaut/game-kit/web/crash/dom/crashScreen`) in art's palette with the words in `src/data/text.ts` before the loading strip, Phaser or any game module runs; it writes nothing to the save, and once the game exists it pauses the game on the first uncaught error. `?dev=1` adds the build label and the error's details.
- **Build label:** the crash screen's details and the foot of the settings card show `BUILD_LABEL` from `src/buildLabel.ts` (the kit's `buildLabel`), whose version and commit come from the `define` that `gameViteConfig` gives Vite, the commit reading `dev` without git.
- **Security headers:** the build writes `dist/_headers` from `src/headers/securityPolicy.ts`: a Content-Security-Policy per page with its inline blocks hashed and Cloudflare Web Analytics' beacon allowed, and a year's caching for hashed build output under `/assets/`.

## Game data

### Entities and bindings

- **Game data:** `src/data/types/` holds the shapes by topic (geometry, bird, behaviour, stage, ui, text, map and art), all exported from its index; gameplay values and player strings live in `src/data/`, with the words the player reads in `text.ts`.
- **Stage element roles:** themes bind shared element roles through `ThemePlan.bindings`. Assembly uses one `bindElement` overlay for repeated backgrounds, local scenery, chunk objects and filled doors. An explicit role without a binding is an error; untagged legacy instances retain their configuration. Bindings select the character and may replace behaviour, lines, parallax, phase or visual skin, merge tuning overrides and set or explicitly clear an introductory scene with `scene: null`. Template placement, dimensions, flags and rooms remain unchanged. `ThemePlan.door` names a required binding role; filled doors use its selected character's dimensions to centre across their slot and stand on its floor. Chunk bounds and seam checks resolve each theme's bindings.
- **Visual skins:** `skin: {art, tileset}` changes presentation while the selected character remains the source of collision, dimensions, hitbox, motion and interaction. A tileset key selects tile art; `null` explicitly selects sprite/image art without falling back to the area's tileset. Sprite art retains its bottom-centred anchor. Collidable skins must preserve the canonical effective hitbox; declared sprite hitboxes are compared after anchoring, tile pieces must match the physical footprint, and skin tiles must exist at the correct tile size. An explicit character change through a binding is configuration, independently of a visual skin.
- **Timed hazards:** a stationary hazard whose every behaviour state carries a `hazardPhase` (`warnedCycle` in `src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts` builds the fixed inactive, warning, active, recovery ring) collides only while active and draws a procedural fill per phase ([`timedHazard.ts`](scenes/timedHazard.ts)). A character's `phaseTiles` names its left, middle and right tiles per phase; once its tileset has every one ([`phaseSkinTiles`](presentation/tiles.ts)) it draws from them instead. `noJumpOver` refuses a placement no wider than a running jump's reach (`REACH.gap`). An instance's `phaseSource` names a timed hazard's id in the same chunk; that follower is inert scenery that shows its character's `phaseAnimations` tag for the source's current state and keeps no clock of its own ([`phaseSource.ts`](rules/phaseSource.ts)).
- **Clocked supports:** a stationary one-way ledge whose every behaviour state carries a `supportPhase` (`awningCycle` in `src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts` builds the fixed out, warning, retracted, extending ring, each state playing the tag of its name) collides only while present or warning, by its state at the start of each step ([`effectiveCollision.ts`](rules/effectiveCollision.ts)). Appearing inside a body never moves or crushes it, and it is never lethal. Neighbours offset by their initial state. Bosses predict its presence only until its current state ends, as for crumbling supports ([`clockedSupportPrediction`](rules/zoneCrumbling.ts)), and movement maps carry it as a dependency of kind `crumbling` that masks decide, never a flyer perch. Its placeholder dims while absent until its sprite exists.
- **Gust emitters:** stationary scenery whose every behaviour state carries a `gustPhase` (`gustCycle` in `src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts` builds the fixed calm, warning, gust ring, each state playing the tag of its name) enters its gust with exactly one force pulse lasting the gust's seconds, so pulses never overlap or gap ([`validateGustPhases`](rules/effectiveCollision.ts)). It never collides; the pulse pushes only Chibi, and movement maps ignore the emitter. A plan's `facing` sets its initial facing, the side it blows toward; omitted, a negative `distance` faces left. Gusts blow left, against the route, and never share a chunk with an encounter or a pursuing boss ([`gustPlacement.ts`](rules/gustPlacement.ts)). A pulse visual with `span` repeats its sprite across the push volume, mirrored when it blows left, each bottom on the volume's bottom ([`pulseSpanArt.ts`](scenes/pulseSpanArt.ts)); until the sprite exists it, and the emitter's placeholder, draw in green.
- **Tethers:** an instance's `tether` names a sprite and, unless the instance orbits, its rail's row as a tile offset above the instance's nominal origin; the layout fixes the rail's y in the world. It is presentation only, one depth behind objects ([`tetherArt.ts`](scenes/tetherArt.ts)): from the object's top centre straight up to the rail, the sprite's `cable` tag repeated end to end and its `hook` tag as the bottom segment resting on the top; or, for an orbiting object, from its host's centre to its top centre at any angle, the sprite's `idle` tag placed every segment back from the top, unrotated. Until the sprite exists it draws as a flat line.
- **Gantry crane:** `GANTRY_BEHAVIOUR` (`src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts`) shuttles a one-way ledge container `GANTRY_DISTANCE` tiles along x at `OBSTACLE_MOVER_SPEED` with `OBSTACLE_MOVER_PAUSE` at each dock (`src/data/obstacleTuning.ts`), so it never crushes. `GANTRY_RIDE_TRANSIT` (`src/data/forcedTransit.ts`) marks the ride: boarded from the `dock` bank, forced over the pit and spent at the `far` bank, each window a column interval of the container's origin. Movement maps leave the container out like every mover; the pit floor is static red terrain.
- **Windmill sails:** an instance's `orbit` is a `circle` about its host's centre. A hazard child orbits a linear-moving solid or ledge continuously, its contact swept along the arc. A ledge child (a sail) orbits a static host of any collision and is inert, so the path alone places it: it translates only, staying level, and carries Chibi by its before and after frames. `stops` holds the turn still for `seconds` at `count` evenly spaced angles per turn, the first at the authored pose from area entry, turning at `angularSpeed` between them ([`orbitAngle`](rules/orbit.ts)). `windmillSailOrbit` (`src/data/obstacleTuning.ts`) turns `WINDMILL_SAILS` against the clock, rising on the right, at `OBSTACLE_MOVER_SPEED` at the sail centres, stopping for `OBSTACLE_MOVER_PAUSE` at each quarter turn. Movement maps and flight perches leave sails out like every mover, boss traversal sees only a sail's current frame, its later frames unknown, and transients pass through sails. A chunk or module with a sail holds no encounter, encounter-role object or pursuing boss, and no sail's swept bounds or spoke comes within the seam footing of a module edge ([`windmillPlacement.ts`](rules/windmillPlacement.ts)).
- **Chandelier:** an orbit of kind `pendulum` swings a ledge child `length` tiles below its static host's centre, the pivot, between `-amplitude` and `amplitude` radians from straight below (short of a quarter turn), at `angularSpeed` radians per second, holding still for `pause` seconds at each end; a positive speed holds first at the left end, and `phase` is the fraction of a whole cycle run at area entry ([`pendulumAngle`](rules/orbit.ts)). It reuses the sails' path-ledge rules: inert, translating only so it stays level, carrying Chibi by its before and after frames, left out of movement maps and flight perches, seen by boss traversal in its current frame only, and passed through by transients; its tether runs the same unrotated segments from the pivot. `chandelierOrbit` (`src/data/obstacleTuning.ts`) swings `CHANDELIER_SWING` at `OBSTACLE_MOVER_SPEED` along the arc with `OBSTACLE_MOVER_PAUSE` at each end. No chandelier shares its module with an encounter-role object or a pursuing boss (the paired module may hold one), and no arc's bounds or chain comes within the seam footing of a module edge ([`chandelierPlacement.ts`](rules/chandelierPlacement.ts)). The windmill's encounter-free chunk rule applies to `circle` sails only.

### Stages

- **Zones:**
  - **Plans and instances:** a zone is played from its `ZonePlan` in tiles (`src/data/types/stage.ts`), converted once to pixels for the rules: areas of terrain and placed instances. Each instance is a character (`src/data/characters.ts`: its art, animations by state, size, words and quirks) on an archetype (`src/data/archetypes.ts`: its collision and default motion, reaction and interaction), resolved once at load into a flat object, an instance's overrides over its character's tuning over its archetype; carried-choice flags place or leave out an instance.
  - **Doors:** Use at a door shows its room (Rooms). Use reaches a door wherever his drawn frame (art's bird frame, placed over his collision box as the zone draws it) intersects the door's rect at all, which is its drawn rect; of two doors under his frame, the one whose centre is nearest his opens (`doorAt` in `src/rules/zone.ts`). Entering a door ends every engaged pursuit in the area as an escape, without an escape bark.
  - **Escapes:** each zone step lists in `ZoneState.escapes` the bosses whose pursuit ended as an escape on that step, each with its owner and a `cause` of `distance` (beyond its escape distance) or `door`; read them when `steps` advances, as barks are. A death step lists none, and a boss's own death is never one.
  - **Edges:** his collision box crossing an area's linked edge automatically enters its named target entry, at any height. An unlinked right edge clears the stage, which fades to black and follows its catalogue completion route; the zone walk follows the same links and never presses Use. His box reaching an unlinked left edge (left of `backX`, the first column's right edge) ends the zone without clearing it (`turnedBack`): it fades to black the same way and opens the map with him on that zone, without completion dialogue, statistics or a stage chooser, preserving completion progress and cumulative totals; through `?zone=` it plays the same zone again. Every zone's start, hand-built and every recipe's, sits clear of the first column, and held left from it he reaches that column, nothing in it stopping him (`src/data/zones.test.ts`), and the zone walk counts a try that takes him into it as failed, so neither ends a zone by turning back. A held key's repeats never press it, so a key held from before the zone resumes counts only once pressed again.
  - **Assembly:** a zone in `RECIPES` (`src/data/zones.ts`) is assembled from the chunks and themes in `src/data/chunks.ts` for a seed drawn afresh each time he enters it (`src/rules/assemble.ts`): its start chunk, a seeded run of its theme's chunks whose seams join, within its inclusive difficulty band regardless of position, each at most its `maxPerZone` and never one twice in a row, then its finish chunk, the whole within its length, searched with backtracking (see below), with its events and doors in seeded slots (recorded in the plan): a filled door slot places the theme's door at its own size, centred across the slot (half a tile in where the widths differ by an odd count) and standing on its floor, leading to its room. Events are not built yet. Each chunk's contents move to its zone columns, its terrain looks map to the theme's tileset, and the theme's repeated backdrops span the zone; the result is an ordinary `ZonePlan`. Optional `ZoneRecipe.chunks` restricts generated middle chunks to its authored pool; omitting it retains the compatible theme pool. The tests check every chunk against the chunk and seam rules, validate each recipe, and walk each chunk alone (`src/data/chunks.test.ts`, `src/sim/chunkWalk.test.ts`), on small fixtures in `src/testing/chunks.ts` as well as gameplay's chunks. The search first remembers each failed state by its last chunk and every limited chunk's uses; on a pool of more than `EXACT_LIMITED_CHUNKS` limited chunks, or past `EXACT_NODE_LIMIT` nodes, it restarts from the same seed remembering states by feasibility alone (chunks alike in columns, slots, encounters, seam rows and `maxPerZone` form a class, counted by uses per class), cuts states no length, slot or encounter bound can complete, and throws past `SEARCH_NODE_LIMIT` nodes. Pools of many single-use chunks, such as certified pairs, take the second search; it draws differently, so it serves only seeds the first cannot finish. `assembleZoneAvoiding` first assembles with a list of chunk ids left out of every middle pool and falls back to the whole pools when no assembly fits without them. A fresh stage entry from the map passes its destination's recent middle chunks (`recentStageChunks` in `src/progress/save/pageSave.ts`); developer sessions, `?zone=` trials, previews and simulations pass none.
  - **Restart:** each restart is a new attempt that rolls the objects' random motion afresh from the zone's seed (`?seed=<n>` fixes it).
- **Ordered stages:** each section declares its whole-column `length` range, including only its applicable global stage caps. One or more weighted compatible middle chunks fill that range; limited chunk counts and nonidentical neighbours apply across the stage. Optional `difficultyWeights` are grade shares in flat and ordered recipes: each chunk's weight is divided by the total weight of its grade in the pool and multiplied by its grade's share, so each grade is drawn in proportion to its share however many chunks it holds; omitted grades keep share 1 and zero excludes a grade. `areaOrder` retains the generated section sequence and each area's `walkLinks` names its automatic left and right stair destinations. Section changes redraw local art and camera bounds, reset held controls and animation pose, and retain the stage clock and attempt. A retry starts at the stage checkpoint. `src/rules/stageProgress.ts` supplies the same current-attempt fraction to the HUD, death card and diagnostics: ordered sections contribute their playable widths, and the bird's centre supplies local progress. Diagnostic cold starts retain the original stage's progress origin while selecting their own retry checkpoint. Bot input and stall detection reset local state at each section change without reseeding decisions or extending tick and trace budgets. Explicit diagnostic goals remain area-local, including a goal crossed immediately before a stair transition. Optional chunk and recipe `rows` declare matching authored heights; omission retains the library height of 60 rows. `spawnRow` selects an actual opening-cap terrain top without shifting terrain or changing seam rows. A recipe's `stageExit` is local to its final cap and resolves to an area trigger; reaching it uses the existing cleared-stage statistics and Continue flow.

- **Chunk modules:** a `ChunkModule` (`src/data/types/module.ts`, values in `src/data/modules/`) is half a chunk, 30 columns by the zone's 120 rows, filling the left half, the right half or either. Two modules pair in a zone when the left one fills the left half and the right one the right half, both list the zone, their joint rows match and at most one holds an encounter (`pairProblems` in `src/rules/modules.ts`). `moduleProblems` keeps every solid, slot and placed thing's swept span inside the module's columns in every theme of every zone it lists, applies the full seam rule at each listed side's outer edge and the joint rule (2 flat columns each side under 6 clear rows, `MODULE_RULES`) at its joint edge, matches `encounters` to its antagonist objects and requires a route target, hazard or mover. `composePair` makes the chunk `${zone}-${left}--${right}`: the right half shifted 30 columns, objects left, right, then each half's `zoneObjects`, slots prefixed `left:` and `right:`, the zone's themes and frame (`ZONE_FRAMES` in `src/data/zoneFrames.ts`: Industrial roof and tunnel walls, Apartment wall bands and interior planes, Woodland depth), `maxPerZone: 1`, weight the product of the halves', and grade the harder half's, one harder when both are 4 or 5, at most 5, unless `PAIR_GRADES` overrides it. Only pairs in a zone's certified list are composed, and `STAGE_RECIPES` (`src/rules/zonePlans.ts`) appends them to its recipes' pools.
- **Arenas:** a `ChunkArena` (`src/data/types/arena.ts`, values in `src/data/arenas/`) is a designed full chunk, 60 columns by its stage's rows, holding its stage's one encounter: `stage` (a `RECIPES` key), `boss` (the character the stage's theme binds to `antagonist`), seam-row `entry` and `exit`, terrain and objects as a chunk's, `refuges` (`cover`, `high-ground` or `low-ceiling`, each a surface spot and width), `routes` (surface waypoints in order; a route whose last waypoint is its first is a loop) and required-route `targets` beyond the encounter profile's. `arenaChunk` (`src/rules/arenas.ts`) makes its chunk in the stage's theme and zone frame with `encounters: 1` and `maxPerZone: 1`. `arenaProblems` (`src/testing/arenas.ts`) requires an `arena-` kebab-case id, the chunk rules, exactly one antagonist object bound to the stage's boss, at least 2 routes with distinct ids and waypoints, one a loop of at least `ARENA_LOOP_TILES` tiles, every waypoint and refuge a spot Chibi reaches from the entry on his skill-free movement map and each route's legs walkable in order, a refuge of a kind `ARENA_COUNTERS` lists for the boss (high ground against charges, cover against Fert's parcels and Pebble's droppings, a low ceiling against Kiwi), seam-row edges and a grade the stage draws. `arenaCoverage` requires exactly one arena per encounter stage (a flat recipe with encounters), every arena's stage an encounter stage and an arena stage's recipe to hold 1 encounter; problems of stages in `ARENA_PENDING_STAGES` are reported and never fail. Only arenas a certified list names are admitted: `CHUNK_LIBRARY.arenas` maps each such stage to its arena's chunk. Assembling a stage with an arena draws a run of its pool's chunks without encounters with the arena's columns set aside, which gives the middle count `n`; the stage seed (stream `arena:<stage>`) draws the arena's index in `floor(n / 2)` to `n - 1`, and the run is searched again with the arena pinned there (then at each later index of that half, wrapping, until one joins), so a retry keeps the layout and a fresh entry moves it. A stage without an arena assembles as before. The evade playtest (`src/testing/evade.ts`) is a skill-free controller (run, jump and flutter; no Dash or double jump) that decides every 0.1 s from what Chibi sees, the boss `BOSS_FAIRNESS`'s 0.25 s reaction delay late (body, facing, pursuit and tell, never its scorer, plan, timers or seed). First match wins: the boss behind him (its body centre a tile or more left of his feet) and more than 6 tiles away, with a path to the exit seam on his map clear of it, sends him to the exit; the map he plans on leaves out only the spans and legs from which his body could touch the boss padded by a tile, never through solid terrain; a visible tell runs the attack's first named dodge from the shared dodge scripts; the engaged boss within 6 tiles or closing faster than his walk sends him to the waypoint on any route that maximizes the boss's map distance minus his, ties by route id (a switch when its route changes); at a countering refuge while the boss is engaged he holds; otherwise he circles the loop, turning when its next waypoint lies nearer the boss. Once the chase ends he walks to the exit on his movement map, waiting while anything thrown is in flight or the boss ahead threatens him. Until the rearm cooldown after an ended chase, a boss whose `pursuit.keepClear` is set never moves toward Chibi while he is within its escape distance (`keptClear` in `src/rules/behaviour.ts`): its horizontal movement toward him is cancelled, and a flyer's vertical movement too. It runs 4 zone seeds for each of 2 starts (the boss acquires him at the entry, or on the loop mid-arena); each run passes with no death, the exit within 60 s of the boss first acquiring him and at least one switch (a move to another route or refuge, or a change of level while following the loop: grounded at one loop waypoint row after another), and an ended chase is not required. Each run also walks the same start and seed with the boss removed; in at least 6 of the 8 runs the exit comes 3 s or more after that boss-free walk's. One sample arena per boss (`src/testing/arenaSamples.ts`, outside the pool) proves the shape, checks, placement and evade (`src/testing/arenas.test.ts`, `src/testing/evade.test.ts`).
- **Arena evidence:** completed arena proofs emit diagnostic JSON on stderr during certification and verification. It separates static and functional results from the approved bot slowdown challenge. Unexecuted stages and human playability are labelled `not_evaluated`; search UNKNOWN counts are `not_recorded` because the run records do not expose them. Evidence does not change qualification criteria, proof hashes or persisted certificates.
- **Certified lists:** `src/data/certified/<zone>.json` lists every middle chunk a zone's stages draw: its whole authored chunks (`single`) and its certified pairs, each with its grade, encounters, slots, a physics fingerprint (`layout`, independent of theme and skin), the hash of its required-route targets and fills (`route`), the FNV-1a 64 hash of its required walks' behaviour trace and their executed steps (`work`). `inputs.data` hashes the data the proofs read; `inputs.proofs` hashes the behaviour trace fixtures, the game-kit release and the tool's version. `npm run pairs -- certify` writes the lists (generated output, committed in its own commit; regenerate after a rebase, never hand-merge): per zone and candidate, the chunk rules, a grade every zone recipe draws, at most one encounter, the fingerprint in each zone theme, the required walks with slots empty and filled in each theme whose physics differ, the boss path-out (`src/sim/pathOut.ts`) for an encounter, then 64 seeds of every zone recipe assembled from its connectors and the list alone. Its arena step proves every arena of the zone in its stage's theme on every certify: the static checks, the required walks of the encounter profile (less targets on terrain the zone frame marks out of reach) with slots empty and filled, the boss path-out and the 8 evade runs (`src/testing/arenaCertify.ts`); a passing arena is listed in `arenas` as `{ id, stage, hash }`, its hash over the arena's data and chunk, the required walks' and evade runs' trace hashes and the game-kit release, and a failing one is left out. `verify` checks each listed arena's stage and static checks, and proves arenas first in its sample, by their hash. It reuses an entry whose layout and route are unchanged unless `--all` or the proofs hash changed, and runs proofs on at most 4 worker threads. `verify` checks every list's input hashes and structure against the tree, fully proves entries that differ from `--changed-since <ref>`'s lists (which needs git), then proves a sample ordered by `--seed` (the lists' input hashes by default, so it rotates as they change), starting only proofs whose recorded work, at the observed proving rate, ends within `--sample-ms` of the start (20 s by default); `report --out <path>` writes counts and rejections. `src/testing/pairCertify.test.ts` checks freshness, structure, that each recipe draws exactly its zone's certified middles, and each zone's distinct layouts against its floor and target (125 layouts split by grade share).
- **Terrain families:** `broad-mesa` and `low-central-crossing` are grade-4 noncombat chunks with ordinary connected routes and explicit required-surface contracts. Normal hostile middle templates use `maxPerZone: 1`; distinct templates in a family remain eligible, and encounter quotas remain recipe data.

### Rooms

- **Rooms:** a door is an instance whose archetype is `door`, with a `room` from `ROOMS` (`src/data/rooms.ts`); the resolver refuses a door without a room and a room on anything else. Leave, back and Escape close it (`src/ui/pause/roomInput.ts`); Tab and the arrows focus Leave, and Enter and Space press it. - **Door context:** ordinary street and Apartment doors retain their 60x84 footprint and nearest-overlapping-door Use rule. Their opening aligns at door offset (-60,-96); the full base must have actual solid support. These images add no collision or random draws and preserve prior object indices. Indoor doors retain opaque walls and trim with window exclusions; park/test prototypes are not resized.

### Scenery

- **Parallax:**
  - **Factors:** decorative backdrops can set `parallax: { x, y }` on an instance or a theme backdrop, each factor from 0 (fixed in the view) to 1 (world speed), with both axes at 1 when omitted. Assembly preserves these factors.
  - **Background catalogue:** outdoor themes may select a reusable [`background catalogue`](data/backgrounds.ts): one fixed weather treatment and independently seeded far/mid sections cover the stage. [`Background assembly`](rules/backgroundSections.ts) appends visual objects after physical objects without consuming terrain randomness or changing existing object indices. Complete authored depth stacks retain their spans and species; generated layers fill only the remaining coverage.
  - **Interior planes:** instances with `backdropPlane: 'interior'` use explicit `col/row/cols/rows` as a fixed world rectangle at depth -3.5, between base walls and placed props. [Interior texture phase](presentation/interiorBackdrop.ts) scrolls inside this two-axis clip, aligned to native tiling at the named `groundAnchor` entry's clamped camera baseline. The quad never moves with parallax or receives outdoor ground-position compensation. Opaque base walls, world-speed props and authored window apertures remain in place.
  - **Layers:** whole-area layers retain camera scroll factors and their full run width. Explicit repeated chunk layers (`col: 0`, `cols: chunk.cols`) narrower than their area use a world-anchored quad with local texture offsets, clipped to the exact assembled chunk span. Their authored source-height and bottom alignment stay intact. Generated outdoor layers set `backdropExtendBottom`, which [assembly](rules/tiles.ts) accepts only on a static, nonphysical outdoor `backdropSpan` without `backdropPlane`, and the renderer only on repeat-X art. It adds a cap: a 1px frame over the native image's final row, sharing its RGBA pixels, at the span's width, texture phase and depth. It fills only the [exposed viewport bottom](presentation/backdropBottom.ts) below the art and is destroyed with its base layer. Indoor planes and ordinary placed scenery do not extend. The renderer updates them before each render, including paused previews and death-camera captures. Placed scenery retains its own art height and bottom anchor while the camera climbs.
  - **Ground anchor:** an outdoor repeated span with a `groundAnchor` entry adds [`G - (1 - py) * C0 - artHeight`](presentation/backgroundGround.ts) to its authored row offset. `G` is the [ground line](camera/camera.ts) at that entry's x, `C0` the clamped camera scroll y with Chibi's feet on `G` at that entry, `py` the layer's vertical parallax factor (1 when omitted) and `artHeight` the layer's height. The reference is fixed per area; it does not follow platforms.
  - **Scenery:** noninteractive static sprites with no collision, contact region, quirks or reaction may also use non-default factors, including wait-only animation graphs without entry actions. `ChunkPlan.scenery` appends these instances after existing objects and filled doors, preserving their object indices and motion seeds. They draw behind terrain, cropped to their chunk; camera compensation does not change animation or facing. Other sprites, tiles and placeholders require default factors.

## Save data

Version-11 [progress](progress/save/progress.ts) uses the kit’s versioned save in `localStorage` under `chibis-way-home.save`. It stores the opening acknowledgement, completed stage-node ids, selected stage, seen destination introductions, owned skill ids, one active skill id (or null) and, per destination, the middle chunk ids of its last `RECENT_STAGES` fresh stage entries from the map, newest first ([`recentChunks.ts`](progress/recentChunks.ts); written after assembly, dropped of unknown ids on load, absent until the first entry, and left out of the next fresh entry's assembly while it fits without them). Boot loads it after startup readiness. Invalid or older incompatible progress is backed up under `chibis-way-home.save-backup` and resets; a future version is left alone. Cold launch opens Start; saved Continue opens the map. Confirmed Restart clears progress once, including skill ownership and selection.

The separate version-7 [statistics store](progress/save/stats.ts), `chibis-way-home.stats`, retains historical stage/complete-visit bests, whole-playthrough deaths, whether a skill was used since the last confirmed Restart (an absent flag counts as unused) and the active-time ledger:

| Display field | Scope                                         |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Last time     | The attempt that just ended                   |
| Best time     | The fastest successful attempt for that stage |
| Attempts      | Since the last clear or confirmed Restart     |
| Stage total   | Cumulative active time for the stage ID       |
| Zone total    | Cumulative active time for the zone ID        |
| Run total     | Whole-playthrough active time                 |

Only confirmed Restart clears the three cumulative totals. Start, Continue, fresh map entry, retries and revisits preserve them. Missing stage/zone ledgers initialize once at zero while preserving other saved fields and the known Run total; future active play accumulates normally. A stage's first entry after its clear or a confirmed Restart starts with attempt count one; Retry and coming back to the stage uncleared reset only the attempt clock and increment the count. Confirmed Restart ends every unfinished stage run. Visit-local summaries remain separate: a zone best requires every catalogue stage in that visit and sums its successful attempt times; incomplete visits cannot set a zone best.

Version-1 [earned achievements](progress/save/achievements.ts), `chibis-way-home.achievements`, use the kit's `earnedFormat` and `earnedStore`: the earned ids and those not yet announced, both in award order, written one call at a time. Loading drops unknown ids instead of resetting. Restart keeps the record, and developer sessions neither read nor write it.

A store written by a newer version (the kit's versioned save status `"newer"`) blocks that store's writes for the session. `loadProgress` reads all three stores and returns `"newer"` if any is newer; it then writes nothing, not even the statistics' totals upgrade of an older store or the stats lifecycle flush, and the boot scene updates the game instead of opening Start (Offline play).

`recordActiveStageStep` counts actual playing steps and records a new death once. Introductory departure counts while play is unpaused; menus, introductory approach/exchange, paused dialogue, cutscenes, death presentation and retry cards do not. Clocks update in memory, checkpoint once per second, and flush on pause, death, exit and page lifecycle events. Where storage is unavailable, saves last for the page session. Settings use separate keys. Developer sessions return zero whole-run totals and suppress normal progress/statistics writes and resets; unknown diagnostic stages do not record real-stage statistics.

The invulnerability checkbox records `invulnerable=1` in the URL, retained when loading another stage. ### Bounded simulations

`npm run simulate -- --help` describes the headless stage runner. Run it only inside the project container. For a small diagnostic run, use `npm run simulate -- --stage street --seed 1 --runs 1 --max-ticks 600 --max-retries 0`. Larger batches require coordination with gameplay; this command is not a routine gate. Both this CLI and `src/sim/playabilityCli.ts` refuse an existing `--out` path.

The four sim CLIs (`cli.ts`, `playabilityCli.ts`, `reachCli.ts`, `tuningCli.ts`) parse with `node:util` `parseArgs` through [`command.ts`](sim/command.ts): `-h` or `--help` prints usage to stdout and exits 0; a boolean is set by its presence and cleared by `--no-<flag>`; a value starting with a dash is passed as `--flag=<value>`, as in `--move=-1`; an unknown, repeated or empty option, a positional argument or a rejected value prints the message, naming the value and the allowed values, with the usage to stderr and exits 2; any other error prints only its message and exits 1. `npm run reaches` exits 1 when a measured reach is off its target.

`src/sim/simulate.ts` exposes `prepareStage(layout)` and `simulateStage(tuning, prepared, config)`. `src/sim/report.ts` batches explicit seeds through a caller-supplied preparation function. Layouts and static sensor indices can be reused between policies. The CLI loads art metadata once, assembles each seeded stage once and performs no rendering; optional navigation remains bounded by its explicit prediction-work limits. Its Node-only asset loader is separate from the pure engine and frontend.

Rollouts call the real `stepZone` at `STEP_SECONDS`, including collision, moving platforms, behaviour patterns and explicit retries. Conversations are acknowledged at the runtime proximity and activate their actors; presentation, menu and retry-card waits consume no active time. The bot does not use doors or make narrative choices. Retry retains the assembled layout and uses the real attempt reseeding. Tick and retry limits bound each run and report exhaustion explicitly.

JSON output includes current-attempt and peak progress, completion, death reasons, retries, active seconds, steps and runtime. Per-run timing measures rollout only; aggregate timing includes stage preparation, and total CLI timing also includes asset loading. Timings vary by machine; deterministic gameplay metrics exclude timing. Active seconds count physics ticks across all attempts. Progress uses the same weighted current-position calculation as gameplay across ordered areas. Coverage lists only visited areas and chunks, plus automatic area transitions; prepared but unvisited floors are not covered. `lastAttemptSeconds` reports only the final executed attempt; `successfulAttemptSeconds` appears only after a stage clear and excludes earlier failed attempts. Prediction steps never count toward active time.

Optional `stopAfterStallTicks` (`--stop-stall-ticks`) ends a policy with no further directional progress as `policy-stall`, independently of tracing and its event cap. Area changes and retries reset that progress window. Allow for moving-platform cycles and inspect final movement, waiting ticks and planner status before interpreting a stop. Optional `maxWallTimeMs` (`--max-wall-ms`) bounds rollout wall time and reports `wall-budget`. Batch execution counts stage preparation against that same per-stage limit; the batch limit (`--batch-wall-ms`) also bounds preparation and dispatch. Unstarted rollouts appear in `omittedSeeds` and per-seed `omissions`, with reason `batch-wall-budget` or `stage-preparation-wall-budget`. Wall limits depend on machine speed and are incomplete diagnostics, not gameplay failures. A rollout that reaches its wall limit reports `wall-budget`, keeps `fullPass` false and omits `successfulAttemptSeconds`. Use an external container timeout to bound synchronous preparation and individual planning calls as well.

The default `quick` navigation profile retains its existing limits. Explicit CLI `--profile validation` selects planned navigation with a 750-second executed-play budget, three retries, 2,000,000 prediction steps across the run, a 30-second stage wall limit and a ten-minute batch wall limit. Its horizon, candidate count, replan interval and 1,200-tick stall limit remain unchanged. It captures a bounded witness and requires a full pass by default. Explicit overrides may lower these limits, never raise them; the quick profile cannot borrow the larger prediction budget. The profile supplies capacity, not evidence that a stage passed. Catalogue execution and replay must fit the authorized overall budget.

Actual deaths can retain up to sixteen local failed navigation choices across retries of the same run, with nearby dynamic phase context. A fresh stage starts with empty memory. Predicted deaths are not recorded as actual failures, and witness replay uses only its recorded inputs without consulting navigation memory.

Optional `witness: {maxSegments}` (`--witness-segments`) records every executed input with lossless run-length encoding, including retry boundaries. Its explicit segment budget cannot exceed the executed-tick budget. Overflow sets `truncated`; it never becomes a full pass. Witnesses include the exact supplied configuration, resolved seeds, source/data identifiers, actual prepared-layout/tuning hash, final area, tick count and retry count. Supply a nonempty source build identifier when validating.

`fullPass` means an actual whole-stage clear in the final ordered area from its normal checkpoint, with a complete untruncated witness and source identifier. Section goals, cold starts, partial coverage and all budget stops remain non-passes. Batch `validation.fullPass` requires every requested run to pass with none omitted. `--require-full-pass` requires an explicit witness budget and exits unsuccessfully when that acceptance condition is not met, while preserving the JSON results.

`replayCompletionWitness(tuning, prepared, config, limits, witness)` separately returns `verified` or `mismatch`. It checks stage/tuning context, source/data identifiers, configuration, seeds and segment counts before executing recorded inputs through the same real-step, introduction and retry path as the original run. It has explicit tick and wall budgets and reports its actual executed steps and runtime. Generating a witness does not claim replay verification. Replaying a catalogue stage requires authorization within the overall evaluation budget; it is never an implicit second run.

Every result includes a terminal position/area/chunk and candidate-object snapshot, per-attempt death snapshots and the ordered observed chunk path, independently of trace truncation. Snapshots and trace events record actual `kinematics` fields: `grounded`, `vx`, `vy` and `carryVx`. These identify review locations and policy limits; nearest-object attribution does not establish the cause of a death or prove terrain impossible. Terminal and requested sampled/stall/death events also capture the latest bounded navigation decision: waypoint, selected action, up to eight candidate scores/rejections and predicted endpoints, predicted versus actual progress, replan/cache state and recovery intent. This is the most recent input decision, while the event position is the actual post-step position. Nearby context contains at most four terrain rectangles and four collidable object bounds within half a screen, with actual positions, motion, initial phase, area time and current behaviour/facing/state time. These bounds remain diagnostic candidates, including where lethal body contact uses a narrower silhouette. No full prediction worlds or per-tick dumps are recorded.

Optional `start` selects an existing `{area, entry}` or a collision-box top-left `{area, x, y}`. Bounds and overlaps are checked against actual terrain and initial live objects. Sections initialize the real area's actors and motion at time zero; they are cold starts, not snapshots of earlier play, and retry returns to that same section. No preceding tutorial completion is inferred.

Optional `goal: {area, x}` stops a section at its configured leading-edge threshold in the movement direction with `goal-reached`, independently of stage `cleared`. Goals must be in bounds and ahead of the start. A death at that tick remains a failure. Without a goal the whole stage remains the target; section goals do not count as stage completions.

Optional `seeds` separates `layout`, `motion` and `bot`; each missing value falls back to the existing `seed`. Batch preparation receives the layout seed, rules receive the motion seed and the bot receives its own seed. Results include resolved seeds, the complete supplied configuration, actual starting position and caller-supplied `provenance.build` / `provenance.data` identifiers. Supply exact non-secret source identifiers when comparing data versions. The Node loader adds an SHA-256 identifier of the actual layout, assembled plan and tuning; the CLI also records the Git revision when available, with explicit `--build-id` and `--data-id` overrides. Git identifiers do not describe uncommitted code. Prepared catalogue stages reject mismatched layout seeds and cache only the most recent layout, bounding cache memory.

Optional `trace` requires `maxEvents` and `stallTicks`; `sampleEveryTicks` additionally samples position and input. The run-wide event cap bounds all attempts together and reports dropped events. Death events replace non-death samples when the cap fills. Each event records attempt/local tick, global tick, area, position, input, chunk index and assembly ID where prepared metadata supplies it, death reason and the nearest or overlapping collidable object's position and behaviour. Object attribution is a diagnostic candidate, including after swept contact, not proof of causation. With tracing omitted the runner creates no diagnostic events or object-attribution scans.

Bot inputs expose reaction ticks, look-ahead pixels, jump-hold ticks, error probability, movement direction and jump policy. The reactive policy probes local support, walls and hazards using indexed terrain and current moving-object positions. Optional `jump: "planned"` selects bounded navigation configured by `skill.navigation`: `horizonTicks`, `maxCandidates`, `replanTicks` and `maxPhysicsSteps` (CLI `--plan-horizon`, `--plan-candidates`, `--plan-replan` and `--plan-ticks`). Planning uses the real engine; its counters report decisions, candidate rollouts, prediction physics steps and budget exhaustion. The prediction-step budget applies to the whole run, including retries and area transitions; exhaustion reports `prediction-budget` unless the executed tick already cleared the stage or ended in death. A planner with no safe candidate or an exhausted budget has not proved the layout impossible. It does not plan a route, schedule enemy phases or search for takeoffs. The supplied diagnostic settings are not calibrated human skill presets; gameplay owns calibration. Failure by this bot is not evidence that a stage is impossible. Diagnostic defaults enable adaptive holds between `minJumpHoldTicks` and `jumpHoldTicks` for low obstacles, retain longer holds for gaps and hazards, and release after reaching a measured obstacle top. These are local geometric estimates, not skipped physics. `waitForMovers` stops movement for a nearby missing moving landing, resumes on live alignment, and caps waiting with `maxWaitTicks`; no predicted platform position replaces real stepping. Legacy caller skill objects can omit these optional controls.

#### Saved tuning reports

`src/sim/tuningCli.ts` post-processes saved case artifacts and a saved structural playability report without running physics, navigation or replay. Run it in the project container:

```bash
node src/sim/tuningCli.ts --cases cases.json \
  --structural structural.json --out-prefix reports/tuning
```

The cases manifest is a JSON array of `{ "stage": "stage-id", "path": "stage-artifact.json" }` entries. Artifact paths resolve relative to the manifest. Each artifact contains `run` plus its `sourceCommit`, optional `readinessCommit` and optional `replay` result. Duplicate stage cases are rejected. The output directory must exist; existing `.json`, `.csv` or `.md` outputs are refused without overwriting them.

The JSON report contains stage input counts, recorded chunk-residence tick intervals, revisits, independent failure locations, source/configuration/seeds/replay metadata, zone totals and the existing structural comparisons. CSV and Markdown separate successful-attempt time from all-attempt active time, show input proportions over captured ticks with coverage labels, list slow and revisited chunks, and reuse structural zone comparisons and visited mechanics inventory. A first-attempt full pass requires a full pass with zero deaths and retries plus a complete, untruncated attempt-zero witness whose retry count is zero; replay status remains separate. Residence intervals use recorded centre-based chunk transition ticks, not sampled trace positions. Input counts distinguish direction, reverse direction, no direction, jump presses and held jump ticks. No-direction input does not establish physical waiting, and these artifacts do not measure distance or airtime.

Structural joins require the same source revision, stage, layout seed, area, chunk index and chunk ID. Missing or mismatched joins stay unknown; structural and runtime hashes cover different domains and remain separate. Witness coverage is complete, incomplete or absent, with unknown input ticks reported explicitly. Mechanics present in visited chunks describe available structure, not observed use. These are bot observations and structural proxies, not human difficulty or fun scores; the successful-attempt duration target remains unmeasured by this tool.
