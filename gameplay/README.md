# Gameplay

Gameplay ownership, tuning targets, playtesting and current release work. Chibi's Way Home is a landscape platformer about Chibi, a lost bird, travelling zone to zone across a map to get home. Phone browser first, with keyboard on desktop.

## Scope

Gameplay owns the rulebook and the values and content in the data value files (every `src/data/*.ts` except `types.ts`, `screen.ts`, the index and the tests). Every number (speeds, jump and flutter heights, timings, counts) and every string a player sees lives in those files, never in code. Reusable behaviour patterns for animals and moving inanimate objects are gameplay-owned values in `src/data/`: their primitives, sequences or states, transitions, timing, animations and tuning. Tech owns the schema, primitives, condition evaluation and shared executor. A new field, key or shape goes through tech.

## Method

- **Bounded diagnostics:** compare pinned source and matching layout, motion and bot seeds with explicit executed-tick, retry, planning-work and wall-time limits. Report successful-attempt time separately from total active time, current and peak progress, observed area/chunk coverage, deaths, policy stalls and omitted cases. Budget exhaustion or an uncalibrated policy failure does not establish impossible geometry or human difficulty; check deliberate waits against mover cycles. A bot clear does not establish human enjoyment or calibrate the human successful-clear time target. Device playtesting remains necessary.
- **Decisions:** the user's. Gameplay asks with a prompt, recommendation first and the trade-off in a line; routine work needs no asking.
- **Design review:** apply the [stage design principles](design.md#stage-design-principles) to required routes, including cross-chunk transitions. Preserve the rulebook’s fresh-entry and same-run retry lifecycle when comparing layouts. Use recorded residence, revisits and input metrics to identify locations for human playtesting, not as enjoyment scores.
- **Design sessions:** start with "design session" and a topic. Claude keeps floated ideas apart from agreed ones, asks the question that unblocks a decision, and on "wrap up" proposes doc changes, writing them once confirmed.
- **Difficulty:** use configurable per-zone difficulty targets and variation bands from `src/data/`, with a demanding first zone and a difficulty increase in every following zone. Vary required climbing chunks, moving obstacles, ground hazards and enemy yards; a seed need not include every challenge type. Every built and future stage, including the first tutorial and final stage, has a fixed opening and ending around randomized middle chunks. Vary meaningful challenge combinations and order within the zone band and valid chunk connections. Run and attempt lifecycle rules are in the rulebook.
- **Jump tuning:** use one uninterrupted decelerating ascent, with a minimum hop for short presses and the full jump on a hold. Place overhead short-jump challenges to match the measured hop. Keep the reaches in the rulebook aligned with the simulator, including coyote time in the running gap reach
- **Phone playtesting:** judge jump feel, touch input and readability on a landscape phone. The user performs device playtesting.
- **Required-route verification:** routine chunk checks use the explicit required-route registry and real physics to reach declared entry-connected targets and clear the exit, with slots empty and filled. Grounded target intervals are enforced where authored. Exhaustive optional-surface exploration belongs to diagnostics and synthetic fixtures, not the production required-route gate. Passing a scoped route check does not establish that every generated stage or a human pacing target passed
- **Tune to the targets:** a result inside its band needs no tuning. The user declares when the goal is met.

## Current scope

The linear route has six themed zones and twenty-one stages. The first five zones retain three stages each; Apartment has six standalone upward floor stages: Lobby, Restaurant, Pool, Spa, Residential floors and Penthouse. The route table in [`design.md`](design.md#v1-route) owns names and IDs. Every stage has fixed one-screen opening and ending chunks around a randomized middle. All twenty-one catalogued stages use 120-row geometry; Blue connects lower open routes to upper challenges, Industrial connects underground descents and ascents, and Countryside connects lower terraces to an upper bank ferry. Woodland adds guarded lower routes and canopy climbs, and Home city adds lower climbing yards. Apartment links its lower zigzag ascent to the retained upper climb, window and exit. Recipe data owns lengths. The park remains a debug prototype outside V1; later scope is listed in [`next.md`](next.md). Completing the route does not declare a V1 release.

- **Abilities:** new games have no owned skills. Gift rooms permanently grant Dash, Double jump or Speedy; repeated grants have no additional effect. Exactly one eligible skill is active: the first grant selects itself, later gifts preserve the current selection, and tapping an owned HUD icon selects it instead of toggling it off. Holding an icon opens paused details; pressing one from Pause opens details rather than selecting. Details replace Pause and close back to play, respecting other modal owners. Ordinary required routes never require dash or double jump. Only selected Speedy improves normal horizontal running and air-control speed; data owns its multiplier. Room gift dialogue and details remain nonmodal. Developer checked overrides add temporary eligibility; unchecked retains saved ownership, and developer selection does not change the normal save. Further upgrades and extra lives remain outside the current mechanics, as listed in [`next.md`](next.md)
- **Apartment floors:** each floor starts at a safe lower checkpoint and ends at an upper automatic stair or elevator exit. Clearing a nonfinal floor shows statistics and Continue before entering the next floor. Doors remain entrances to optional special rooms. Death Retry restarts only the current floor in the same generated run
- **Boss traversal:** all six bosses acquire Chibi from their current shared chunk or when both physical body centres are inside the actual current viewport, without requiring line of sight. Missing chunk metadata does not disable the viewport alternative. Attack-specific sight checks and actual on-screen bark presentation remain separate. During an attack tell, an outward red-and-ink outline follows the current sprite’s alpha silhouette without changing its body or contact. Named antagonists retain their lethal contact and specials with a six-screen original-anchor planning leash. Pursuit ends beyond the current 60-tile two-dimensional boss-to-Chibi distance, without a timed unreachable give-up. Each attack has a visible data-timed tell and a completion boundary before its shared cooldown; pursuit can continue during cooldown. Chase speeds derive from Chibi’s normal run so they can catch up; ground movement uses bounded physical walking and jumping across ledges, blocks and gaps. Reacquisition distinguishes immediately connected ground from jump routes requiring a physical planning proof; an unknown or exhausted proof is not an unreachable verdict. Dedicated arenas and combat remain outside V1
- **Dialogue and encounters:** the first five zones introduce their antagonist once per playthrough in stage one; Apartment introduces Fert in Spa, its fourth floor. Earlier Apartment floors contain no physical Fert and do not mark his introduction seen. Each introductory stage has no normal hostile encounters. Its approach and manual conversation pause play; as soon as the antagonist starts running away, the introduction releases its own hold and Chibi can move. Other pause or modal owners still block play, while the noncolliding cinematic actor continues offscreen outside the encounter quota. The first five zones have exactly two randomized middle boss encounters in stage two and exactly three in stage three. Apartment has exactly two in Residential floors and three in Penthouse, with none in its first four floors. Fixed caps have no normal hostiles. Paused conversations advance manually without a timer. Spotting, escape and scheduled Fert barks stay live for up to five seconds. A dialogue-panel tap uses Continue when present; otherwise it dismisses only the current bark, never the whole paused exchange. The rulebook owns retention
- **Entry and retry:** app reopening shows the start screen, with Start alone until an actual catalogued stage has been entered. Map browsing, chooser cancellation and returning to the title do not enable Restart. Actual stage entry enables Continue and Restart; confirmed Restart restores the beginning state and plays the opening before the map. New game also plays the opening, while saved Continue bypasses it. Completion or Skip marks it seen; interrupted unseen playback restarts at the first beat. Its four beats advance manually with no mandatory animation wait, and Skip ends the entire opening. Manual title Continue shows Chibi on the map without a modal. Only manual map Continue opens the stage chooser. Entering from the map starts a fresh run; death Retry restarts the same stage directly in the same run. Pause and Resume preserve position and run. Walking left from a stage entrance returns to the map; Apartment floors use their lower entrance edge.
- **Selection:** up to three full-size side-by-side cards per page show actual scene screenshots with slight scenery differences within the zone's palette. Locked stages remain visible, grey and disabled. The latest available stage is the highlighted default. Main names retain the standard font size; smaller, lighter stage-number labels sit at the top right. Screenshot mappings derive from zone and stage order.
- **Transitions:** every clear shows a full-page summary with a centred 240 × 120 px actual stage preview above a 336 px-wide statistics group. Last time, Best time and Attempts form the left column, with Attempts at the bottom; Stage total, Zone total and Run total form the right. The stage summary has no divider, background fill or separate bottom Whole run row. Retry retains its compact centred modal, current groups, divider and actions. Native fonts, spacing and the reserved bottom-right Continue footer remain. Nonfinal stages then enter the next stage directly without the map. A zone’s final stage shows the outgoing exchange with Skip, then statistics before the map walk. Fert’s final exchange and statistics lead to the existing celebration. Clearing a stage unlocks the next built stage; replays preserve unlocks.

Current code calls an individual stage `ZonePlan`.

Generated-run statistics count the initial attempt and reset for a fresh generated run. Death Retry accumulates attempts in the same generated run; historical successful bests remain. A whole-zone best requires successful clears of every catalogued stage from one actual visit. Partial visits cannot set a zone best or mix historical stage bests. The displayed active-time totals accumulate until confirmed Restart: Stage total covers the same stage ID across all generated runs, retries and revisits; Zone total covers all stages and visits of that zone; Run total covers the whole playthrough. Fresh map entry, stage entry and saved Continue never reset these totals. An initial store starts at zero. Confirmed Restart clears all three totals while preserving historical bests. Attempt-local bookkeeping and death/completion counts remain separate from these displayed times. The rulebook owns field definitions and active-time exclusions, including paused conversations.

## Targets

Targets are assessed per stage and player skill. Bands other than the successful-clear time target remain to be agreed with the user. The kinds of target:

- **Encounter distribution:** satisfy the rulebook's encounter quotas across each zone’s catalogued stages, with distinct encounter sections, ordinary platforming between them and varied layout. Encounter spacing is flexible; it is not a time-based encounter rate
- **Stage length:** target about 60 seconds of successful active play on the ordinary required route in the first five zones with no powerup active. Apartment retains about 90 seconds per floor (about nine minutes total). Dash, Double jump and Speedy are never required on ordinary routes. Because the first grant automatically selects a skill, no-powerup calibration uses an allowed test harness or an ungranted save, not a new player-facing off toggle. Active-time calibration remains limited. It is a tuning target, not a hard time limit or a direct conversion to tile length. Track this generated run's total active time including retries separately from the historically fastest successful-attempt time, using the rulebook's active-time exclusions
- **Reaches:** Chibi makes every jump at a reach in the rulebook's Grid, and never one tile beyond it. A pass or fail check, not a band
- **Restarts:** a failed attempt is one that ends in death (a fall, hazard or crush), followed by the retry choice. Restarts per stage, by skill: some attempts fail, and the band says how often
- **Route spread:** every route a choice opens lands within a narrow window of the stage's median clear time and restart rate, so no route is a lot easier or harder
- **Session length:** the time to finish the game, by skill

### Boss playtest criteria

For each placement, record the source, seed, starting support, active skills and whether entry is natural or staged. Check ordinary no-powerup forward passage, boss traversal and escape routes, visible tells, actual effects and contact, supported recovery and the next attack boundary. Include neighbouring terrain, cover and exits rather than checking an isolated animation alone.

Report observed outcomes separately from unobserved specials, incomplete recovery, controller limitations and exhausted work. A cooldown is not a contact grace period. Record any declared reaction delay and whether the cue was actually visible; automated success does not establish human fairness or enjoyment. Compare distinct physical encounter layouts rather than counting cosmetic aliases as variety.

## Active obstacle implementation targets

The transfer-gate crossing is implemented, with bounded real-physics qualification of its approach, boarding, gate crossing, landing and recovery. The warned vent is implemented, with bounded real-physics qualification of its refuge observation, inactive-window crossing, active-phase death, warning retreat and retry reset. Crumbling support is implemented, with bounded real-physics qualification of its landing warning, ordinary jump escape, removal, restoration, waiting fall and retry reset. None establishes a forced ride or whole-stage completion. These are active implementation targets, not optional ideas or unscheduled Next scope. Preserve current stage lengths, movement, encounter quotas and ordinary routes without skills. Existing ferries, gates, low ceilings and orbital hazards remain the starting primitives.

### Crumbling support contract

A terrain run or a stationary object opts in with a `crumbling` profile; behaviour, orbit and motion overrides are rejected. Warning (0.8 seconds) and absent (2 seconds) durations live in `CRUMBLING_SUPPORT_TUNING` in `src/data/obstacleTuning.ts`.

- **Trigger:** a new solver-resolved grounded-support contact by an actor with traversal physics (Chibi, or a boss standing or landing on support) starts one warning. Ground movers without traversal physics, movers on fixed tracks, projectiles such as falling blocks, overlap, flybys, side contact and decorative objects never trigger it. The warning latches when the actor steps off; later contacts neither restart nor extend it, and simultaneous actors start one warning
- **Warning and absence:** warning leaves normal collision and the full skin intact and draws cracks over the support's opaque face. At warning expiry the support, its skin and its lip are removed for the absent duration, counted from the removal boundary. The support itself is nonlethal and never becomes a red hazard
- **Restoration:** after the absent deadline, the support waits until its full volume is free of Chibi, every behaviour-driven hazard body and every transient, including falling blocks. It never materializes through a body, restarts the absent clock or adds an effect while waiting. It restores intact and disarmed; restoration never triggers a warning, but a later genuine landing does
- **Reset:** Retry and area entry restore every support intact with no recorded contacts
- **Fixed ticks:** prior deadlines are processed at each fixed-tick start, before colliders are built. A deadline takes effect at the first boundary at or after its active-clock time, and pause freezes that clock. Warning expiry wins over a new contact at the same boundary. Collider state stays constant through the tick. Support contacts resolved during a tick take effect at the next boundary
- **Occupancy:** from the absent deadline, the full volume is tested against current bodies once at each fixed-tick start until clear. Runtime occupancy is always known; a prediction whose occupancy is unknown is unknown, never safe
- **Prediction:** runtime and boss prediction share the same clock, ordering, contacts and occupancy. Each support's presence is known only until its earliest possible change; later presence is unknown, never safe
- **Route:** the crumbling support crossing places a 6-tile ledge over a red-floored pit between two fixed banks. An ordinary jump from the ledge reaches the far bank within the warning, without a required skill or altered physics. A Chibi who waits on the ledge falls into the pit

### Forced-transit attack deferral target

This approved target awaits source, adapter and authored-range qualification. Only explicitly authored, certified mandatory-ride metadata may opt in; moving support, velocity or an endpoint alone is not a certificate, and unknown geometry does not create one. Do not add per-tick planning to infer forced transit. Existing ferries without a certified forced interval remain non-opt-in.

- **Boarding:** only boarding from a bank the ride's metadata lists can start a protected leg; other boardings follow the ordinary attack rules. Boarding starts unprotected, with attacks allowed, until the ride's forced window is certified
- **Deferral:** defer only Pebble's new targeted swoops and aimed poop bursts until Chibi is offered the first safe forward exit or useful refuge. Other attacks and untargeted hazards continue. Retreat alone does not end the episode. Once a forward opportunity has been offered, camping does not rearm deferral. Pebble may reposition safely overhead while deferring; when an attack becomes eligible, use its normal full tell
- **Disembark:** any safe grounded contact, authored bank or not, ends the protected leg, even while a ride still supports Chibi
- **Existing danger:** committed attacks, existing projectiles, body contact and red hazards remain unchanged and lethal. Deferral is not invulnerability or a general moving-platform attack ban
- **New leg:** after safely getting off at a middle bank listed for a second ride, boarding any ride that lists that bank starts a fresh protected leg. Hopping directly between rides never re-arms protection. A hop from a still-deferring leg directly onto another ride keeps deferral until Chibi reaches safe ground; the second ride's exits never end the first leg
- **Qualification:** certify that an ordinary Jump, any release from tap to full hold, can begin on the next valid input, allowing one fixed-step release prefix if Jump is held. Preserve actual carry, steering, coyote time and collision. Do not count intentional grounded dwell, a run-up or waiting for a future dock as immediate availability. Scope the proof to this criterion, not an impossibility claim about every future controller
- **Unknown state:** while the ride state is unknown, attacks follow the last certified state. Unknown never certifies a forced window or proves an exit unavailable

## Release and tuning

**Vertical layout:** All twenty-one catalogued stages have four-screen capacity (120 rows, 1440 px). Blue’s required route connects lower open traversal, a lift climb, an upper red-hazard gate and a surface return; Industrial connects underground descents and ascents; Countryside climbs ordinary terraces to an upper bank ferry before returning to the surface. Woodland connects guarded lower routes, canopy lifts and red gates; Home city connects climbing yards to upper routes. All zones retain the shared 60-column chunk width, existing stage lengths, IDs, encounter cadence and doors; ordinary routes require no skills. The six Apartment floors connect a new lower zigzag ascent to the retained upper climb, window and automatic exit, preserving gift rooms and encounter cadence. Woodland’s world-coordinate wildlife follows shifted terrain footing without shifting the authored slower vertical depth reference. This geometry capacity does not establish whole-stage completion or human pacing.

**Current implementation scope:** all six antagonists are always lethal on contact, periodically attempt a direct charge or body contact, and have distinct specials with data-driven frequencies. Archie adds a leaping pounce; Pebble pairs contact swoops with falling white-block poop; Trixi pairs ground rushes with visibly arming red-edged traps; Bini pairs charge and maul with the finite growl gust; Kiwi pairs ground charges with a vertical drop; Fert pairs charges with thrown parcels. Pebble's blocks disappear on landing, and Bini's push stops outside the gust or behind cover, with high ground the primary avoidance. These rules are paired with the matching runtime, values and art. Jump rules and the unresolved friendship outcome are unchanged.

All twenty-one V1 stages are built. Successful-clear active-time calibration remains limited; landscape-phone checks of jump feel, touch input and readability remain.
