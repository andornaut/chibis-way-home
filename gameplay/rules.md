# Game rules

The play loop, controls, interface, progression and saves. See [movement and skills](movement.md) for Chibi’s physics and skills, and [player copy](player-copy.md#dialogue) for dialogue and tips.

## Core loop

1. **A stage:** travel through one place, mostly left to right and bottom to top: platforming and talking with animals.
2. **Between stages:** Continue enters the next stage directly. At a zone's end, the outgoing exchange and statistics lead to the map walk or final celebration. See [map progression](#map).
3. **Home:** Chibi returns to the shared apartment.

## Rules

| Term               | Meaning                                                     |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Zone               | A themed map destination containing catalogued stages       |
| Stage              | The playable level, represented by `ZonePlan` in code       |
| Encounter section  | A distinct enemy encounter segment within a stage           |
| Chunk              | A hand-designed unit used to assemble the randomized layout |
| Opening and ending | The fixed caps around a stage's randomized middle           |
| Run                | One generated stage layout and its seed                     |
| Attempt            | One try to complete the stage within a run                  |

## Screen and controls

- Landscape, 720×360 base resolution. Phone browser first, with on-screen touch controls; keyboard on desktop. In portrait on a touch screen, the game draws turned a quarter turn to fill the screen, so the player turns the phone
- **Keyboard:** A, H or Left moves left. D, L or Right moves right. Space, W, K or Up jumps. E, F or Enter uses. P opens or closes the pause card. Escape follows the [dismissal priority](#settings)
- **Mouse:** on the unclaimed playfield, the left button is Jump and the right button is Use. Hold or release the left button to control jump height as with keyboard Jump. Left click keeps its ordinary action on UI buttons. Right click activates an available action at its pointer target in the foreground interface. A UI press stays claimed through release, drag or hold. Suppress the browser context menu only when the game consumes the right click. Native controls and editing keep their usual mouse behaviour
- **Overlay:** the move hint and the touch buttons show only when the primary pointer is coarse (a touch screen); with a fine pointer, such as a mouse, neither shows. On a touch screen both show by default. Settings hides each set on its own, and hidden controls still work.
- **Touch buttons:** Use sits above Jump at the right thumb. Pause or Menu sits at the top left and the skill discs mirror it at the top right. Keep controls inside the 2:1 view and safe insets, with symmetric side clearance and non-overlapping targets. Use moves with Jump. Jump's target reaches the lower-right corner. Buttons animate on press. Exact geometry lives in `src/data/layout.ts` and the [touch controls guide](../art/style-guide.md#touch-controls)
- **Touch movement:** a touch anywhere except on a button or other interactive element sets the neutral point where it lands. Dragging left or right from it past a small dead zone moves Chibi that way at full speed; back inside the dead zone, he stops. Releasing ends the movement
- **Move hint:** the left marker mirrors Jump's resting position and has no input target. During drag it shows the neutral point, then returns to rest on release
- **Use and dash:** Use opens an available room door that Chibi's drawn frame overlaps; of several, the one whose centre is nearest his body's centre. Otherwise, with Dash selected, a fresh press starts a short horizontal dash in the current movement direction, or his facing direction when there is no movement input; with no door and no Dash selected, Use does nothing. The direction stays committed through the dash. Dashing works on the ground and in the air, has a cooldown and allows one air dash until Chibi lands. Dashing does not change gravity or the jump curve. Terrain blocks the dash, and lethal contacts stay lethal; it grants no invulnerability. Tuning lives in `src/data/bird.ts`

## Interface

- **Action holds:** every discrete pointer tap or click action also performs the same action on a 500 ms hold by default, unless an explicit hold override is declared. A completed hold fires once and suppresses the release click. Availability, focus, foreground, readiness, drag, cancellation and disposal guards remain authoritative. Skill holds retain their explicit details action. Native keyboard activation, editing controls, native selects and continuous movement, range or drag input retain their existing semantics
- **Action rows:** centre a button when it is the row's only visible action, including Close, Back, Retry or a primary action. Hidden actions do not count. With multiple visible actions, retain secondary actions on the left and the primary on the right; vertical menus retain their primary action at the bottom
- **Accessible names:** game controls use player strings for their accessible names. Name the Start and Opening regions, map destinations and current location, rooms, celebration and progress indicators. Read conversations by speaker and announce captions, barks, notices, tips and connection messages without moving focus. Hide drawn touch controls from screen readers
- **Button states:** game buttons, including startup Retry and crash Reload, mark focus, press and selection through visible changes to face fill, text or icons, with system colours in forced-colour mode. Preserve the ordinary frame across states and omit added outlines, border rings and ring-shaped shadows. Pressed movement can supplement colour. Other controls retain their authored visible focus treatment
- **Focus:** focus stays in the top physical card and returns to its opener or play. Install steps start on their close X. Achievements, Choice, Conversation, Death, Pause, Restart, Settings, Skill details, Stage select, Stage summary and Update start on the card. Fresh Enter or Space with no action focused chooses Try again on Death or Continue on the map. In the foreground interface, W, K or Up navigate up, A, H or Left navigate left, S, J or Down navigate down, and D, L or Right navigate right. Tab keeps its existing focus order. E, F, Enter or Space activates an available focused action, including an eligible HUD button that already has focus. During play, Tab skips Pause and skill discs. Bare play uses the gameplay bindings. Native editing, composition and keys owned by controls such as sliders keep their existing behaviour
- **Consistent:** the same element looks and behaves the same on every screen: top bars, panels, close controls, buttons, headings. Follow the shared [Pause and Menu navigation policy](https://github.com/andornaut/game-coordinator/blob/main/docs/design/game-policies.md#pause-and-menu-navigation): screen and modal titles stay horizontally centred near the top and unobscured by controls
- **Disabled controls:** pressing an ordinary disabled control refuses activation with shared shake and red-label feedback. A locked stage card applies the refusal across its face, screenshot and both labels; keyboard selection skips locked stages. With Reduced motion on (see Settings card), the refusal uses steady red feedback without shaking or flashing. Refusal never selects an answer or dismisses a card, and respects modal blocking, fresh-release and cancellation guards
- **Exact:** spacing sits on a fixed pixel grid: equal margins, aligned edges, and text clear of its box's edge by the same amount on both sides
- **Minimal:** show only what the player needs. No heading that repeats a tab's name, no label the picture already gives, and no decoration that competes with play
- **Options:** art mocks up visual options for the user to pick
- **Press areas:** a standard button’s press area adds a share of its shorter side to each edge, within the margin bounds and at least the minimum target size in `HIT_RULE` (`@andornaut/game-kit/runtime/ui/hitAreas`). Jump and Use use their own touch regions, skill discs use a fixed `SKILL_HUD.target` square in `src/data/layout.ts`, and a full-row control uses its row as the target. For grouped HTML controls, neighbouring targets stop midway between faces. A card applies this rule across its button groups, full-row targets and footer. For other overlapping targets, the nearer button wins
- **Stage progress:** a thin bar the view's width stays fixed along the screen's bottom edge and has no input target. It shows Chibi's current horizontal position as a fraction of the stage's width, rather than the furthest position reached, so climbing does not move it; on death it shows his death point. Backtracking lowers it; Retry resets it to the stage's beginning
- **Switch states:** centre the visible state label below each switch in Settings and [Cheats](cheats.md)

## Map

- **Availability:** all six V1 zones appear in route order, with their catalogued stages as listed under V1 route. Unbuilt zones and stages are visibly unavailable and cannot be entered. The park is a debug prototype outside the V1 map
- **Backtracking:** replaying an earlier stage preserves later unlocks
- **Between stages:** clearing a stage marks it completed and shows an image-backed statistics interstitial with Continue centred as its sole footer action. After a nonfinal stage, Continue enters the next stage directly without showing the map. A zone's final stage first shows the outgoing zone exchange, then current-stage and cumulative zone statistics, before returning to the map and automatically animating Chibi walking along the route to the next zone. After the walk, the map waits for the player's selection; it does not enter that zone automatically. Completed stages and opened paths are marked. The final stage follows the celebration rule instead
- **Buttons:** Back to title is on the left; Continue is on the right and is the default keyboard action
- **Celebration:** only clearing Apartment building's sixth stage, Penthouse, shows Fert's outgoing exchange and the final statistics interstitial followed by the finish celebration, including on replay. Continue returns to the map; Restart asks first, as on the start screen
- **Forks:** V1 is linear and has no route forks or laps
- **Progression:** Parcel depot is unlocked from the start. Clearing a stage unlocks the next built stage in the route table. Clearing a zone's final stage opens the next zone. Unlocked built stages remain available for replay
- **Selection:** only manually pressing Continue on the map opens the selected zone's stage-selection modal. Up to three full-size side-by-side cards per page stay in catalogue order, including locked stages, which remain grey and disabled. Previous and Next controls change pages; the modal opens on the page containing the highlighted latest available stage. Keyboard navigation can focus unlocked stages across pages or activate enabled page controls. Each card shows an actual scene screenshot, the main stage name at the standard font size, and a smaller, lighter Stage {number} label using its catalogue ordinal at the top right. Names never shrink to fit
- **Walk:** Chibi stands on a zone. Left and A step left. Right and D step right. Tapping an available zone also moves him there. Up, Down and Tab move focus through the footer actions and include Menu at the ends. Continue selects the zone he stands on

## Runs and attempts

- **Continue:** reopening the app shows the start screen. Manually choosing Continue shows the map with Chibi on his saved zone, without opening a stage chooser or entering a stage
- **Entry:** entering a stage from the map, replaying it or continuing through a statistics interstitial generates a new layout
- **Pause:** pausing and resuming keeps the same run and position
- **Restart:** a full restart resets progress to its beginning state; the next actual stage entry starts a fresh run
- **Run links:** `?zone=` with a stage’s map node plays that stage outside the map. After a clear or a turn back, it re-enters the stage directly. A clear records its run statistics and clear achievements like a map clear. If the map already opens the stage, the clear also saves its completion and stands Chibi there. Otherwise, the map stays as it was. Turning back does not save a clear

## Stage statistics and interstitials

Every clear, retry and zone-end presentation separates current-stage statistics from cumulative times:

| Group         | Field       | Meaning                                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Current stage | Attempts    | Attempts in the current stage sequence. The first entry counts as one. Retry and re-entry after an uncleared exit add one. |
| Current stage | Last time   | Active play time in the attempt that just ended                                                                            |
| Current stage | Best time   | Historically fastest successful attempt for this stage, excluding failed-attempt time                                      |
| Totals        | Stage total | Active play time for this stage ID across all generated runs, retries and revisits since confirmed Restart                 |
| Totals        | Zone total  | Active play time across all stages and visits of this zone since confirmed Restart                                         |
| Totals        | Run total   | Active play time across the whole playthrough since confirmed Restart                                                      |

- **New runs:** a fresh generated run starts its attempt-local bookkeeping at zero. Death Retry preserves that run and starts the next attempt. Fresh map entry or replay generates a new seed; entering a stage left uncleared also starts its next attempt, resetting only the attempt time, while entering a cleared or not yet entered stage starts at one attempt. Confirmed Restart ends every unfinished stage run, so each next entry starts at one attempt, and keeps historical best times
- **Presentation:** the full-page stage summary centres a 240 × 120 px actual stage preview above a 336 px-wide group of six statistics, with a divider separating current-stage statistics from aggregate totals and no background fill. The left column reads Last time, Best time and Attempts, with Attempts at the bottom; the right reads Stage total, Zone total and Run total. Keep native fonts, spacing and the reserved footer with Continue centred as its sole action. Do not duplicate zone attempt or best-time columns
- **Statements:** retry and stage-transition statistics show one statement from the current stage's zone antagonist, selected from the zone's retry or clear line. This is a static stats statement, not a live world bark, so it does not require an on-screen world actor. There is no back-and-forth exchange, Skip or additional action: retry has Back to title and Try again, and stage transitions have Continue. Present it once per actual stats entry, not again on rerender, modal resume or an action click; a new death creates a new retry entry
- **Whole run:** only confirmed Restart clears Stage total, Zone total and Run total, and it starts fresh run statistics; a new stats save, or one without stage or zone totals, starts them at zero and keeps its other data. Fresh map entry, stage entry, retries, revisits and saved Continue preserve them. Paused time is excluded; time in a stage's gift rooms counts toward its attempt and every total. Clear presentations snapshot completion after recording the cleared stage
- **Zone visits:** saved, not shown. Visit-local attempt and best-time bookkeeping is separate from the cumulative displayed Zone total. Zone last-attempt time sums the latest finished attempt time from each visited stage; zone run-total time sums all active attempt time in those current-visit stage runs; zone attempt count sums their attempt counts. A zone best requires successful clears of every catalogued stage in the same visit and sums only those successful-attempt times. Compare it only with other complete visits. Partial visits keep their completed-stage count, set no new zone best and never combine historical stage bests into a fictitious full-zone result

## Death and retry

- **Active time:** count only time spent playing the stage, including time in its gift rooms. The introductory approach and paused exchange do not count. Active play resumes when the antagonist starts its departure; ordinary pause and modal exclusions still apply. Pause, other paused conversations, menus, the death camera pan, sequence and card, floor-transition presentation, interstitials and map travel do not count. Floor transitions preserve the same run clock; its attempt and total times follow Stage statistics and interstitials
- **Card:** after the animation, hold the final pose beneath a required modal showing the zone and stage, current-attempt progress percentage, the three current-stage fields and the three aggregate totals, in two three-row columns either side of a divider. Back to title is the secondary button on the left; Try again is the primary button on the right. Keep the card compact and centred horizontally and vertically in the viewport. Keep the camera framing established before the death animation; opening the modal does not reframe Chibi or replay the animation. The card stays until a button is pressed; outside taps, back and Escape do not dismiss it
- **Progress:** the card's progress percentage is the stage progress at his death point, not the furthest point reached
- **Retry:** death ends the attempt. Try again restarts the current stage directly from its checkpoint in the same run, without the map or stage chooser, as another attempt preserving its seed and generated layout; retries are unlimited. Back to title opens the title screen. There is no permadeath
- **Sequence:** begin the visible camera pan immediately when the death trigger is handled, before the first death pose. Centre Chibi's full authored sprite frame horizontally with its final bottom one tile (12 logical px) above the viewport bottom, preserving the authored padding rather than framing the silhouette and leaving visible context below him. Resolve the presentation's landing support once at the post-recoil position. The distance-based pan uses the timing in `src/data/death.ts` and completes before the death animation starts; a zero-distance pan completes immediately. Keep this camera fixed through one recoil, fall, squash, bounce and seated animation, then open the modal once. A void death retains the full visible 96 px fall from above that final resting position. The world, player input, gameplay physics and active-time counting remain held throughout the pan and death presentation. Pan time never advances the death animation. On retry, title exit or shutdown, cancel any pending pan completion callback and contact-camera feedback before restoring the original camera; repeated restoration has no further effect
- **Storage:** historical best times use a separate stats save

## Achievements

There are 26 achievements in three tiers, in this catalogue order:

| Tier   | Achievements                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Easy   | Boss escapes for Archie, Pebble, Trixi, Bini, Kiwi and Fert; zone clears for Blue cityscape, Industrial outskirts and Countryside                                                          |
| Medium | Zone clears for Woodland, Home city and Apartment building; All gift rooms; no-death third stages for Archie, Pebble and Trixi; no-death zones for Blue cityscape and Industrial outskirts |
| Hard   | No-death third stages for Bini, Kiwi and Fert; no-death zones for Countryside, Woodland, Home city and Apartment building; Home with no skills                                             |

- **All gift rooms:** collecting each of Dash, Double jump and Speedy from gift rooms over the journey. Entering a different zone clears the usable inventory but keeps this collection record and earned achievements
- **Boss escape:** the first escape from that boss. An escape on the same physics step as a death or an area change does not count
- **Home with no skills:** finishing the journey without ever using a skill since the last confirmed Restart: no dash, no double jump, and Speedy never selected. Owning a skill without using it, and owning Speedy without selecting it, do not break it
- **No-death third stage:** clearing the third stage of that boss's zone (for Fert, Penthouse) on its first attempt (see New runs: a death or an uncleared exit uses it up)
- **No-death zone:** clearing every stage of the zone, each on its first attempt, in one continuous sequence with no return to the map and no restart between stages
- **Zone clear:** clearing that zone

- **Card:** the Achievements card lists all achievements, up to six per page. Previous, the page count and Next sit centred in the footer, with Back at the right edge. It sorts them by tier (Easy, Medium, Hard) and keeps catalogue order within each tier, whether earned or not. A locked entry shows its full title and description in muted grey with a grey badge; an unlocked entry has an ink title and a yellow badge. A locked no-death zone shows the current sequence's count of stages cleared, such as 1/3, which resets when the sequence breaks
- **Storage:** achievements use a separate save, like historical bests, and Restart preserves them
- **Unlock:** an unlock shows a single nonmodal message for about 3 seconds with no buttons. It uses the dialogue panel with the achievement icon on the left, the title as a bold first line and the description below it. Unlocks queue and show one at a time, ahead of any tips (Tips) in the same panel, and a tap on the panel dismisses the one showing. Cinematics, rooms, modal cards and active barks defer it; a deferral hides a showing notice and holds its remaining time

## Start screen

- **Achievements:** open it from Menu or gameplay Pause. Back restores the invoking card and its focus
- **Back:** back with no card open goes to the home page in a browser tab and closes an installed app that can close; in an installed app that cannot close it does nothing
- **Boot:** the game opens in landscape. It opens on the start screen, except when a save is newer than the game (Newer save). It never opens automatically on the map or straight into a zone
- **Exit:** at the far top right only in an installed app window that can close, detected when the page loads, where it closes the window and never navigates. Browser tabs and installed apps that cannot close hide Exit
- **Newer save:** Chibi uses the shared [newer-save recovery policy](https://github.com/andornaut/game-coordinator/blob/main/docs/design/game-policies.md#updates) when the progress, statistics or achievements save requires a newer game. Start stays closed during automatic recovery. The travelling loading bar shows while the newer build downloads; switching requires a complete, hash-verified build. Offline, when no newer version is available or when the switch is blocked, the game shows one short message asking the player to connect and retries by itself when the connection returns or the game is shown again. Gameplay start, incompatible save loading and all save writes stay blocked until the new document proves compatibility; continued incompatibility keeps the fence and every save untouched
- **Restart:** on the start screen after an actual catalogued stage has been entered, and on the celebration. It asks first on a card titled Restart with a short question and a close X, Cancel on the left and Restart, which confirms, on the right; Cancel, the close X, back, Esc, P and an outside tap cancel. Cancelling changes neither progress nor action visibility. Confirming resets progress and the opening acknowledgement, then plays the opening before showing the map with him on the first zone and nothing completed. Restart clears Stage total, Zone total and Run total while preserving historical bests and achievements. Confirmation is handled once; repeated input cannot repeat the reset
- **Settings:** available only inside Pause or Menu, never as a standalone icon
- **Start:** before an actual catalogued stage has been entered, Start plays any unseen opening, then opens the map with him on the first zone. Browsing the map, cancelling the stage chooser, returning to the title and reopening the app do not count as starting gameplay: Start remains the only play action, and Restart stays absent. Actual catalogued stage entry enables Continue, which opens the map where he stands without a stage chooser, and Restart beside it. There is one progress save
- **Updates:** follow the shared [update policy](https://github.com/andornaut/game-coordinator/blob/main/docs/design/game-policies.md#updates), with Newer save as the only automatic switch while the game runs. The first install downloads and verifies the complete build before play on the one loading bar, after which the game plays offline; Retry after a failed install resumes at the same progress. When the device cannot store the build (no install support or no storage room), the game plays from the network instead, shows a short nonblocking notice and is not available offline. Later builds stage in the background, reusing unchanged files, with no loading bar and no failure message. A complete staged build becomes active silently at the next launch, before the game or its saves exist. When staging completes during a session, an Update now card appears once over whatever is showing after play has begun, pausing play. It is a compact card titled Update available with secondary Cancel on the left, primary Update now on the right and the ordinary close X. Update now waits for pending saves, then switches to the staged build without downloading; a failed switch hides the card and play continues. Cancel, the close X, an outside tap, Escape and back dismiss it: play continues on the current build, the card does not return this session and the next launch switches silently
- **Update checks:** the shared scheduler receives a check request on accepted Start, Continue, restart confirmation or stage selection, and when returning to the start screen from another view. Refused, repeated or cancelled actions do not request another check. Returning to the start screen checks in the background. If staging finishes on Start in the same session, the Update now card can appear there and activation requires confirmation

## Settings

- **Back, Close and Resume:** follow the [shared navigation policy](https://github.com/andornaut/game-coordinator/blob/main/docs/design/game-policies.md#back-close-and-resume). Back returns to the opener. Close dismisses the top dialog. Resume returns from Pause to play. X, outside tap and browser Back dismiss one layer where allowed, preserving Cancel, save guards and required choices. Each distinct Escape press first goes to the topmost visible foreground surface. It invokes a visible, available Skip in dialogue. Otherwise it uses that surface’s allowed X, Back or Close. A blocking surface consumes Escape. Pause or Menu is the fallback only when no foreground surface handles or blocks Escape. Held repeats never dismiss another layer. With no card open, browser Back over eligible content opens Pause or Menu. Firefox for Android's installed app and browsers without CloseWatcher before first interaction may handle Back themselves
- **Back to title:** goes to the [start screen](#start-screen) from any zone, the map or a card
- **Modals:** centre each modal card horizontally and vertically in the logical game viewport. Its backdrop covers the full UI layer, including the letterbox bars. Every modal card, and the gift room's card, declares a width family: Large 688 px (Achievements, Death, the gift room's card, Settings, Skill details and Stage select), Medium 500 px (Cheats, install steps, and Pause and Menu) or Small 400 px (the celebration placeholder, the connect status message, dialogue choices, Restart and Update). A card takes its family's width, or the view width less a 16 px screen margin on each side where that is narrower. Its family is the smallest whose width fits its content and whose height fits the view less 16 px above and below. Height follows the content with 16 px of shared padding and the same screen margins above and below. If the body needs more room, it scrolls while the footer stays in place. Preserve the standard text and control sizes; do not shrink them to fit a card. Every titled modal card has no title bar: its full title is centred across the full modal near the top, with symmetric clearance for the full close hit target and its gap on both sides. A card without a visible title uses an accessible label. A closable card has a close X at its top-right corner. Its full press target stays clear of body controls. Keep the full standard font size: wrap the title or grow the card when needed, never shrink the text. Every actual modal card dims its background and blocks all underlying game, native, page and window controls, including keyboard input. Only the top physical modal receives input. Covered HUD controls and the shared entry stay painted but dimmed and inactive beneath it. Single-message barks are nonmodal and keep gameplay interactive; full-screen story and room pages are not modal cards. A tap anywhere outside a card that can close only closes it, as its close X or back would, the margins beside the game included; on a card with no close (a required choice or the Death card), it does nothing at all. A press that drags does nothing
- **Open:** the shared entry control keeps its position on touch and fine-pointer devices. It shows Pause only when the frontmost active content accepts pausing, and a three-line Menu otherwise; Start and the map always use Menu. Nonpausable story, covered content and already-paused scenes use Menu, while initial loading has no entry. Once the foreground Opening page is ready, its logical story-input scope permits the shared entry while no physical modal covers it. A physical modal blocks the background entry and its shortcuts. Opening Pause or Menu holds the active content scenes, preserving input scopes, focus and already-paused scenes. Closing restores the previous content and focus, and continues only the scenes this visit held. P, Esc, back and a frame turn respect the top input owner and its existing navigation guards; a physical conversation, required choice or departure card keeps its own input and does not open a background menu or select an answer
- **Pause and Menu card:** pausable active content uses Paused as its heading and Resume as its primary action; Menu uses Menu and Close, never Resume for an unpaused screen. The card dims and holds its content, with a close X. Its buttons all have one shape. Pause orders Achievements, Settings, [Cheats](cheats.md), Back to title and Resume. Menu orders Achievements, Cheats, Settings, Back to title when present and Close. On Start, the four actions form one stack without Back to title. Elsewhere, the other four actions fill two columns in reading order, with Resume or Close centred on its own bottom row. Achievements, Cheats and Settings return to the same card and mode. The covered HUD and shared entry remain dimmed and inactive while the card or a nested physical modal is open
- **Settings card:** follows the shared [Settings presentation policy](https://github.com/andornaut/game-coordinator/blob/main/docs/design/game-policies.md#settings) and opens from Settings inside Pause or Menu. The left column shows Display joystick, then Display Jump and Use buttons, with their [inline control icons](player-copy.md#voice). Both switches default to on, show Shown or Hidden and explain that touch input still works when the controls are hidden. The right column shows the inline FPS counter toggle aligned with the other settings, then Reduced motion. Reduced motion shows On or Off and describes its reduction of screen shake, flashing and feedback motion. Its first setting on a device follows the device's reduced-motion preference and it then keeps the player's choice. The FPS counter is a device preference separate from saved progress, and is Off by default. On shows frames per second in the bottom-right corner of the device viewport, inset from the safe area and drawn above all layers, including letterbox bars. Install is centred below the switches in the right column and appears in browser mode until the game is installed. It opens the browser's install prompt where available or a separate card with that browser's install title and steps (`TEXT.settings.installSteps`). The steps wrap at the standard text size, and their card grows to fit. Fit all Settings rows and its footer without scrolling. The build label sits above the sole footer action, a centred standard Back button. Back, the close X, P, Esc, browser back and an outside tap dismiss Settings to its opener. The Install steps card’s close X, P, Esc, browser back and an outside tap return to Settings
