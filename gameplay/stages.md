# Stages and routes

The V1 route, authored terrain, stage assembly and room access. See [map progression](rules.md#map) for entry, replay and Continue.

## Stages

- **Appearance:** stage scenery differs slightly while preserving the zone's palette and visual style
- **Camera:** on the ground he sits about two thirds down the screen. In an assembled stage, the ground line follows the chunk he is in, blending across half a screen at each seam. The camera rises only when he climbs above the middle, and never shows below the lowest ground. Every catalogued stage guarantees some appropriate visible depth and retains plain chunks elsewhere. Farther decorative layers scroll more slowly, clipped to their selected chunk; Woodland tree layers cover horizontal challenges and terraces while vertical climbs remain plain. Apartment floors reveal depth through the fixed upper landing window, while ordinary rooms, the exit marker backing and doors keep opaque walls; exterior scenery does not appear through those walls. Ground props stay attached to the terrain
- **Challenge:** platforming, hazards and choices. Routes use ledges and cliffs, not ladders, with both short-press jumps beneath low ceilings and full jumps
- **Checkpoint:** the start of a stage is its only checkpoint. Falling or touching a hazard enters the death flow. Progress is saved: the stages he has completed and where he stands on the map. A stage left before its end is played again from its start
- **Crush:** a moving platform that pushes him sideways carries him to its leading edge. If it squeezes him into terrain or another solid, he is crushed and enters the death flow, as a hazard does. Layouts leave room beside every moving platform's path so he is never pinned on a required route
- **Difficulty:** per-zone bands and grade shares in `src/data/` set a demanding first-zone base and increase difficulty in every following zone. Blue cityscape selects grades 3 to 4, Industrial outskirts and Countryside grades 3 to 5, and Woodland, Home city and Apartment building grades 4 to 5. Each zone draws each grade at its own share, however many chunks that grade holds; larger shares for harder grades distinguish successive zones even when their bands match. Seeded variation remains inside each band
- **Doors:** optional room doors lead only into rooms; marked main-route stairs automatically link stage areas and are distinct from room doors. Use enters a door when any part of his drawn frame overlaps the door's drawn art; where he overlaps more than one, the door whose centre is nearest his wins
- **Edge hazards:** a common solid bright red strip, 4 px thick, can cover the top, bottom, left or right edge of terrain. Terrain hazards and floor traps use red contact edges. Pebble's falling white blocks are lethal projectiles. There are no spikes. Touching any edge hazard enters the death flow. Actual red hazard contact shakes the world and Chibi through the main camera while the HUD stays fixed. With Reduced motion on (see Settings card), the cue is a steady border with no world shake. Animal contact, white blocks, falls and ordinary crushes do not trigger the red-contact cue
- **Encounters:** a stage has at most one gameplay boss encounter, held in its designed arena. In the first five zones, stage one contains only the cinematic introduction, stage two has no boss encounter and stage three has one. Apartment has no physical Fert or introduction in Lobby, Restaurant or Pool; Spa contains only the cinematic introduction; Residential floors and Penthouse each have one. Cinematic introductions are outside the gameplay encounter quota, and the actor starts leaving before normal play resumes. No antagonist is placed in a fixed opening or ending cap or in an ordinary middle chunk. Every second-stage opening has a protected first half-screen, then a mandatory noncombat obstacle, with its door and left map exit accessible. Generated runs vary layout, and death retries retain the same run, arena position included
- **Exit:** reaching a stage's authored exit clears it; stages without one clear at the right end of their final area
- **Going back:** walking all the way back to the stage's left edge leaves it without completing it: the screen fades and the map shows with him on that stage's zone. No completion dialogue, end-stage statistics or stage chooser opens. Completion progress is unchanged; a fresh stage entry starts a new generated run without resetting accumulated totals, and coming back to a stage left uncleared starts its next attempt. Every stage starts him clear of that edge and has no wall in front of it
- **Kinds:** indoor, outdoor, or a hybrid with both
- **Layout:** every built and future stage, including the first tutorial and final stage, has a randomized layout assembled from hand-designed chunks: a fixed start chunk, a seeded run of chunks whose entry and exit heights match, then a fixed finish chunk. Each Apartment floor is one continuous stage area with its own fixed caps. The rules for chunks are under Chunks
- **Length:** target about 60 seconds of active play for a successful ordinary required-route clear in the first five zones with no skill active; Apartment floors target about 90 seconds. These are tuning targets, not hard timers. Dash, Double jump and Speedy are never required for the ordinary route. Recipe data owns each stage's filled length range, including its fixed caps; the debug park keeps its own recipe
- **Scale:** outdoors, the scenery is drawn at a quarter of indoor scale; Chibi, the tiles and the reaches stay the same everywhere
- **Shape:** areas scroll sideways and up, with width defined by their recipes. All twenty-one catalogued stages use a four-screen vertical capacity (120 rows, 1440 px). Blue connects lower routes and upper challenges; Industrial connects underground descents and ascents; Countryside connects terraces and an upper ferry; Woodland connects guarded lower routes, canopy lifts and red gates; Home city connects lower climbing yards and upper routes. Apartment connects its lower zigzag ascent to the upper climbing route, window and automatic exit. Indoors, to a small bird the ceilings are high: a room door is 7 tiles tall
- **Valleys:** every authored production valley has a physical floor with an explicit red lethal contact surface. The hazard is part of the terrain rather than an invisible fall threshold. Required crossings retain their safe banks, platforms and guarded middle ledges; off-world fall handling remains a safety fallback

## Stage design principles

- **Encounters:** build each arena around observing and responding to its antagonist's special attack, with reachable refuges and exposed routes that make the choice meaningful
- **Horizontal crossings:** use compact 22-tile horizontal ferry bank gaps that require platform assistance for an ordinary single jump. Dash and double-jump shortcuts are allowed, but neither ability is required to board, ride or reach the far bank. Fit horizontal tracks to the banks, retaining shared mover speeds and endpoint pauses, 60-column chunk widths and stage-length budgets. The [shared crossings](../src/data/chunks.ts) and [orbital crossings](../src/data/orbitHazardChunks.ts) own track distances and docking geometry
- **Learning:** teach a demand clearly, vary it, then combine it with another demand. Introduce the response before requiring it under combined pressure
- **Moving platforms:** author and validate the complete approach, boarding, cycle, transfer and recovery sequence, not only jump reach. Validate and clearly present the available recovery options after missed boarding or transfers
- **Obstacle combinations:** combine moving platforms and hazards around visible observation positions and supported refuges, preserving ordinary no-skill approaches, landings and recovery
- **Primary demand:** each challenge has a clear required action. Judge variety by the primary route, including required climbs rather than optional upper detours; decorative or bypassable obstacles do not establish a new demand
- **Readability:** show the hazard, approach and safe response before commitment. Provide observation positions and usable refuges so success does not depend solely on reacting after a short attack tell
- **Rhythm:** alternate pressure with recovery that permits observation and preparation for the next challenge. Preserve meaningful movement rather than filling stage duration with repeated waits
- **Variation:** vary required actions, not just chunk IDs. Repeated demanding sequences need variation in their required response; inserting a connector or changing a skin does not by itself provide that variation
- **Zone identity:** give each zone a recognizable combination and rhythm of traversal demands and antagonist interactions while using the shared layouts and physics

## Chunks

- **Assembly:** every chunk, including fixed opening and ending caps, is exactly one screen wide: 720 px or 60 columns. Areas scroll vertically; all catalogued stages use 120-row chunks; debug chunks use their authored heights. A stage's recipe names its theme, start and finish chunks, a length range (start and finish included), a configurable difficulty band, and the events and doors to place. The seed varies the middle chunks within that band, without requiring difficulty to rise; the start and finish chunks are exempt. It never picks the same chunk twice in a row, uses each chunk at most its `maxPerZone` times, avoids the middle chunks of the zone's latest 2 stage entries (`RECENT_STAGES` in `src/data/stageProgress.ts`) while a layout still fits without them, and fills the slots
- **Background coverage:** every outdoor area has continuous interesting background coverage, including vertical challenges. Independently randomized visual sections supply coherent far and middle layers beneath the zone's fixed sky and weather treatment, preserving complete authored layers, tree species and background animals. Selection does not change terrain, physical object indices or gameplay randomness. Opaque interiors retain their authored base walls, props and framed depth windows. Apartment interior depth textures repeat only inside the exact fixed authored opaque wall bands: only the texture phase moves relative to the area-entry reference, not the wall bounds. The window aperture stays uncovered. V1 uses one consistent weather treatment per zone
- **Challenge variety:** the shared middle pools include required climbing routes, moving ledges and red ground strips. Connected climbs combine successive ordinary jumps with supported landings; controlled descents use readable lower passages and grounded recovery to limit overflight. Mandatory challenges include climbs, moving ledges, overhead and vertical red surfaces, and blocks comparable to Chibi's size. The primary route never has more than one screen of unchallenged flat travel, including across chunk seams. Blue cityscape has grade-four returns from upper routes, and later zones retain grade-four and grade-five low-ceiling challenges within their bands. Generation varies challenge combinations and order within the zone's difficulty band, preserving valid connections; a stage is not guaranteed to contain every challenge type
- **Difficulty:** 1 to 5. 1: walking and taps. 2: full jumps and still hazards. 3: gaps near the reach, single moving platforms and single low-ceiling tap spots. 4: timed platforms or moving hazards on the required path. 5: compounded tap-only, timed or platform-to-platform challenges
- **Fairness:** routine isolated chunk checks use explicit required-route profiles, with slots empty and filled. Real-rule witnesses must connect from the actual entry to each declared required target and clear the actual exit; specified target intervals require grounded contact within their authored bounds. Optional surfaces are not required-route obligations. Exhaustive surface exploration remains a diagnostic and synthetic-fixture check, while no-cross checks protect movement constraints and recipe assembly contracts protect generated layouts. Gameplay may use one bounded stage playtest after major changes to answer a concrete reachability question. Exhaustive whole-stage searches are not a routine gate
- **Seams:** a chunk's entry and exit are the ground rows at its left and right edges, as authored in its matching profile, including the deeper Industrial connectors. The seam surface is terrain and runs at least 4 tiles in from the edge, with no gap there, and nothing (no solid, ledge, hazard or moving platform) in the 6 rows above it. Initial unacquired placements and autonomous patrols respect this clearance; acquired pursuit and committed recovery may cross seams while respecting actual terrain and hazards
- **Shared layouts:** every zone uses the same layout templates and object/archetype library. Theme configuration supplies terrain, structures, props, enemies, doors, triggers, backgrounds, parallax and recipes. Obstacle mechanics stay shared engine pieces, but a zone's pools may hold obstacles no other zone uses
- **Slots:** marked regions where the seed places an event or a door. A chunk must pass with every slot empty
- **Themes:** a theme sets the tileset, terrain looks and repeated backdrops for every chunk of that theme, so seams never show. A chunk can still place its own scenery. The park is an outdoor prototype theme

## Objects

Everything in a stage except its terrain is placed in data through archetypes, characters and instances.

- **Animals:** during normal gameplay, every animal in the main stage foreground, apart from Chibi, is an enemy and always lethal on actual body contact, including idle, tells, attacks and rest. Antagonist contact closely follows the drawn body footprint of the current animated pose; transparent frame space around wings or tails and gaps between drawn parts cannot kill on a visual near miss. Frame boxes may bound the contact check but do not make transparent space lethal. Chibi's movement and platform physics and the explicit red-hazard and projectile collider contracts are unchanged. Every antagonist regularly attempts direct charge or body contact and has a distinct recurring special attack; attack cadence and brief recovery windows are configured in data. Counting only engaged time when an attack is eligible, each boss starts a charge or special tell within 20 seconds and its distinct special tell within 40 seconds. While a boss is engaged, attack-ready, not in a chase or pace pause, within its 12-tile engagement range horizontally and has a movement-map path to Chibi, a tell starts or its body comes within `threatRadius` (3 tiles) of him at least every `maxThreatGapSeconds` (12 seconds), both in `src/data/bossFairness.ts`. Animals have no permanent red contact outlines. Archie, Pebble, Trixi, Bini, Kiwi and Fert remain the hostile foreground cast; decorative Woodland wildlife has no contact, interaction or gameplay reaction
- **Archetypes:** a behaviour: collision (none, solid, one-way ledge, hazard or Use), movement, reaction, interaction (such as talking on Use or a quip on approach) and default tuning
- **Behaviour patterns:** reusable patterns in the data choose behaviour primitives, their sequence or states, transition conditions, timing, animations and tuning. Animals and moving inanimate objects use the same composition approach. Code implements the primitives, condition evaluation and shared executor; character-specific cycles are composed in data rather than hardcoded in code
- **Characters:** who something is: one archetype plus tuning overrides, its art and animations, hit box, size, its dialogue and quip lines, and quirks. No character inherits from another. Props (platforms, hazards, doors and backdrops) are characters with art but no voice
- **Crosswalk signal:** Blue cityscape's crosswalk is a stationary strip one row tall on the road between two flat kerbs, wider than a running jump, that repeats a fixed cycle: walk, warning, traffic, then recovery before the next walk. Durations are `CROSSWALK_SECONDS` in `src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts`. Contact is lethal only in traffic, with the red-hazard cue; walk, warning and recovery contact is harmless. The strip shows zebra bars on dark slate while harmless, gains an amber border during the warning and fills red in traffic. A pedestrian signal on the near kerb follows the strip's state with no clock of its own: Walk only during walk, a flashing hand during the warning and Stop through traffic and recovery. Walk leaves time to cross from kerb to kerb, and the warning leaves time to finish the crossing from its middle. Bosses die on the strip in traffic. The crosswalk fills a chunk's right half, only after a planter half where Chibi can watch a full cycle; after any other half it compounds that half's challenge with the crossing, a grade 5 outside Blue's band. Its required route waits on the near kerb for the observed start of Walk, under the user-approved exception to the route budget whose measured ticks its test records. Retry restarts the cycle from walk
- **Crumbling supports:** a stationary ledge or terrain run that Chibi lands on cracks for a warning, keeping its collision, then disappears for an absent duration. Durations are `CRUMBLING_SUPPORT_TUNING` in `src/data/obstacleTuning.ts`. The warning cannot be restarted or extended. After the absence it restores intact once no body or falling object overlaps it; restoration itself never starts a warning, and a later landing starts the next one. Bosses, other movers and projectiles never trigger it. The support is never lethal. Boss prediction knows a support's presence only until its earliest possible change; later presence is unknown, never safe. Retry and area entry restore every support intact. The crumbling support crossing places a ledge over a red-floored pit between two fixed banks: an ordinary jump from the ledge reaches the far bank within the warning, without a skill, and a Chibi who waits on the ledge falls into the pit
- **Decoration:** friendly animals are allowed only as muted background flavour, never in the main stage foreground, or inside rooms reached through doors. Woodland deer stay fixed on actual ground while idling and grazing. Swallows make one autonomous flight arc from first actual on-screen visibility, then disappear without reversing or retriggering during that area visit. Wildlife uses ordinary world camera movement, never follows Chibi, and remains decorative rather than a physical actor. Pausing freezes the flight clock; retry or area entry resets it. Room animals remain in place
- **Gantry crane ride (Industrial outskirts):** a container hangs by its cable from a gantry rail over a red-floored pit 22 tiles wide, beyond a running jump, between two 4-tile banks. Its lid is a one-way ledge level with both banks, so docking into Chibi's body never pushes or crushes him. It shuttles between docks flush with each bank at the obstacle mover speed, 4 tiles per second, pausing 1 second at each dock (`OBSTACLE_MOVER_SPEED` and `OBSTACLE_MOVER_PAUSE` in `src/data/obstacleTuning.ts`), a 10-second cycle; area entry starts it at the near dock, where its first pause lasts 2 seconds. Chibi boards at the near dock, rides across and steps off at the far dock. The ride is the only way across and cannot be skipped; a missed boarding waits on the near bank for the container's return, at most one cycle. The ride is certified forced transit (`GANTRY_RIDE_TRANSIT` in `src/data/forcedTransit.ts`): only a boarding from the near bank starts a protected leg, forced over the pit, and the far dock is its forward exit. The pit floor is ordinary lethal red terrain. The container stays out of boss movement maps, so no boss rides it
- **Instances:** one placement in a stage: a character, a position, size and phase, last-word overrides, its own lines or dialogue scene, a door's room, and flags for carried choices
- **Movement:** an algorithm (static, linear, ease, hop or wander), a direction, a distance, a speed, a pause at each end, and a random variance that rolls fresh on every attempt
- **Orbital hazards:** a red contact hazard can orbit an actual floating platform as the host moves horizontally or vertically. Its continuous circular path follows the host's current position, and contact along the travelled arc is lethal. Boarding, riding and transferring require safe docks and timing around both the host's travel and the orbit; the orbit does not replace the host's collision or motion
- **Overrides:** flow one way: an instance overrides its character, which overrides its archetype. Everything resolves and is checked when the game loads
- **Ravine gusts (Woodland):** a gust emitter at the far side of a ravine repeats a fixed cycle: calm, a leaf-stream warning, then a gust, with durations `GUST_SECONDS` in `src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts`. The gust blows left, against the route, across a 21-tile band 4 rows tall from the emitter's base, and pushes Chibi at 14 tiles/s only while he overlaps it, so walking into it still advances him and leaves no momentum once he is out. Solid cover between him and the emitter blocks it, and above the band is safe. It is never lethal and carries no red: at worst it pushes him into a pocket of the ravine, which climbs back out. It pushes no boss, and a chunk with a gust places no encounter or pursuing boss. The band stays at least 4 columns from every module edge, and area entry and retry restart the cycle at calm
- **Reaction:** none, avoiding Chibi, or approaching him, within a range
- **Retracting awnings:** Home city's awning is a stationary one-way ledge, 8 by 3 tiles, hanging from a facade wall on its left. It repeats a fixed cycle regardless of Chibi: out, warning (a shudder and partial roll-up), retracted, then extending. Durations are `AWNING_SECONDS` in `src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts`. It holds Chibi while out or warning, and he falls through it while it is retracted or extending. Extending inside a body never moves, lifts or crushes it, and the awning is never lethal. Adjacent awnings are offset in phase by their starting state. The awning row hangs two awnings across a drop between two runs; the first starts extending and the second in its warning, so the second comes out during the first's warning and one awning always holds Chibi. Neither the entry run nor the lower street reaches the far run, so the crossing needs an awning; a miss lands on the lower street, whose step climbs back to the entry run. The row fills a chunk's right half, after a stoop half where Chibi can watch the cycle, and its required route fits the route budget. Bosses never trigger an awning or perch on one; boss prediction knows its presence only until its current state ends, and later presence is unknown, never safe. Retry and area entry restart every awning in its starting state
- **Static art:** a behaviour state can use `animation: null` for static art, including a moving inanimate object. Motion and transitions still come from its reusable behaviour graph
- **Swinging chandelier (Apartment):** a one-way chandelier rim swings on a fixed arc below a ceiling mount and stays level. It holds for 1 s at each end, flush and level with the bank there, and swings between the ends at 4 tiles/s along the arc. Chibi boards during the near hold, rides across and steps off during the far hold. It is never lethal; a miss drops him to a floor under the arc that climbs back to the near bank. Bosses never stand or perch on it, and thrown or dropped objects pass through it
- **Warned vents:** a stationary 4 by 6 tile red-contact hazard repeats a fixed cycle: inactive, warning, active, then recovery before the next inactive phase. Durations are `WARNED_VENT_SECONDS` in `src/data/obstacleBehaviours.ts`. Contact is lethal only while active; inactive, warning and recovery contact is harmless, and the drawn phase always matches the contact phase. The contact rectangle is filled dark slate while harmless, gains an amber border during the warning and fills red while active; colours and border width live in `src/data/hazardPhaseStyle.ts`. The warned vent terrace places one vent on a middle deck with grounded refuge on both sides, where Chibi can observe a full cycle; the warning leaves time to retreat to the near refuge. Retry restarts the cycle from inactive
- **Windmill sails (Countryside):** four one-way ledges ride a fixed wheel against the clock, rising on the route side at 4 tiles/s at the sail centres, stopping 1 s at each quarter turn; they stay level, are never lethal and carry Chibi. He boards the sail stopped at the bottom from the lower approach and steps off the sail stopped at hub height on the rising side onto the exit bank, which is beyond a jump from the approach. A miss drops him to the trough under the wheel, which climbs back to the approach. Sails are never perches, bosses never rely on later sail positions, traps and parcels pass through them, and a windmill module holds no encounter. The wheel's bounds and spokes stay at least 4 columns from every module edge

## First stage

- **Ending:** the fixed finish has the final short-jump ceiling, red-edged gap and canopy
- **Layout:** the tutorial introduces the parcel depot of the blue cityscape with a fixed opening and ending around seeded hand-designed street chunks. The middle uses matching city scenery and the zone's difficulty band, with varied climbing and moving-platform chunks
- **Opening:** the fixed start has the opened parcel on safe ground, depot jump lessons and a staged Archie introduction before normal gameplay
- **Rooms:** the street door leads to its assigned skill gift room
- **Route:** the main path continues right toward the city boundary; all three Blue stages connect a lower open route to a lift climb, an upper red-hazard gate and a return to the surface, without requiring any skill. Fixed caps, doors, stage IDs and lengths are defined by their recipes. Streets is the second stage and unlocks when this tutorial is completed; City boundary is third

## City boundary

- **Encounter:** City boundary's one Archie encounter is its designed arena
- **Layout:** the third Blue cityscape stage is randomized from a fixed start, seeded middle chunks and a fixed finish. It uses the street backgrounds and sidewalk terrain, with steps, required climbing routes, moving ledges, red ground strips, red-edged gaps and low-ceiling short-jump challenges inside the zone's difficulty band. The finish has sparse facades and an open outward view
- **Replay:** entering from the map draws a fresh assembly seed; retries keep that layout. Completing Streets unlocks this stage; unlocked stages remain available for replay
- **Rooms:** one door in the fixed start leads to its assigned skill gift room

## Industrial outskirts

- **Appearance:** purple industrial scenery uses floor, crate and container art, shared moving platforms and red-edged inanimate hazards. The deep descent passes windows looking into water. The route through the tunnel stays dry and uses ordinary movement
- **Encounters:** Freight terminal's one Pebble encounter is its designed arena. Pebble uses the shared detection and pursuit rules
- **Layout:** Warehouse district, Loading yards and Freight terminal each have fixed openings and endings with randomized middle chunks, inside the zone's difficulty band. Connected descent and ascent chunks link surface and underground traversal inside the four-screen vertical capacity. Required climbs, moving ledges and red-edged hazards use shared layouts and primitives, with no required skill. Stage lengths, IDs and doors are defined by their recipes
- **Progression:** clearing City boundary opens Warehouse district; clearing Warehouse district opens Loading yards, then Freight terminal. Unlocked stages remain replayable
- **Rooms:** doors lead to skill gift rooms; these stages add no new plot

## Countryside

- **Appearance:** muted yellow countryside scenery distinguishes Farmland's soil and hay from River crossing's banks and timber. Water is decorative scenery, not an implicit contact hazard; only explicit red surfaces are hazards, and falling follows the normal death rules
- **Encounters:** River crossing's one Trixi encounter is its designed arena. Trixi uses the shared enemy detection, pursuit and pacing rules
- **Layout:** Farmland, Country lanes and River crossing each use fixed openings and endings around shared randomized middle layouts within the zone's difficulty band. The required route descends to lower traversal, climbs through eight ordinary five-tile terraces, crosses an upper bank ferry and returns to the surface. The ferry uses the shared bank-gap, platform travel and red-bed challenge. No skill is required; caps, doors, stage IDs and lengths are defined by their recipes
- **Progression:** clearing Freight terminal opens Farmland; clearing Farmland opens Country lanes, then River crossing. Unlocked stages remain replayable
- **Rooms:** doors lead to skill gift rooms; these stages add no new plot

## Woodland

- **Appearance:** woodland uses its canonical green theme through shared terrain, structure, prop and background skins. Water does not add hidden hazards
- **Encounters:** Ravine's one Bini encounter is its designed arena. Bini uses the shared detection, pursuit and pacing rules
- **Layout:** Forest paths, Deep woods and Ravine each use fixed openings and endings around shared randomized middle layouts within the zone's difficulty band. Guarded lower traversal connects to canopy lift climbs, upper red gates and a surface return through the common object and layout system. Grounded wildlife follows its actual shifted footing, while slower vertical decorative depth retains its authored reference
- **Progression:** clearing River crossing opens Forest paths; clearing Forest paths opens Deep woods, then Ravine. Unlocked stages remain replayable
- **Rooms:** doors lead to skill gift rooms; these stages add no new plot

## Home city

- **Appearance:** City outskirts, Back streets and Familiar neighbourhood use the canonical peach theme through shared terrain, structure, prop and background skins
- **Encounters:** Familiar neighbourhood's one Kiwi encounter is its designed arena. Kiwi uses the shared detection, pursuit and pacing rules
- **Layout:** City outskirts, Back streets and Familiar neighbourhood each use fixed openings and endings around shared randomized middle layouts within the zone's difficulty band. The middle challenges sit on a lower route: ground challenges, compound lift, low-ceiling shuttle and high-return chunks, platform orbits and a vertical red gate. Stairs guarded by moving red gates descend from the surface to the lower route, drop deeper below it and climb back; a lift ascent and an upper red-gate ascent rise from the lower route to the upper route, and a red-gate return descends to the surface
- **Progression:** clearing Ravine opens City outskirts; clearing City outskirts opens Back streets, then Familiar neighbourhood. Unlocked stages remain replayable
- **Rooms:** each stage's door leads to its skill gift room: Dash in City outskirts, Double jump in Back streets and Speedy in Familiar neighbourhood

## Apartment building

- **Checkpoint:** each floor is a standalone stage with one checkpoint at its lower beginning. Death restarts that floor on Try again in the same generated run
- **Encounters:** see [Fert’s encounter rules](bosses.md#fert-encounter)
- **Goal:** reaching the upper stair or elevator exit activates the floor transition automatically. Nonfinal floors show stage statistics and Continue before the next floor. Penthouse shows Fert's outgoing exchange, stage and zone statistics, then the celebration, including on replay
- **Layout:** fixed one-screen openings and endings surround randomized one-screen middle chunks within the zone's difficulty band. Every floor shares one opening layout; Penthouse's opening has the same layout before its own wall of tall skyline windows. Apartment uses 120-row stages, with a safe lower spawn and a zigzag ascent connecting to the upper climb. The upper window and automatic exit sit in their authored positions; ordinary routes require no skill. Stage IDs, lengths and gift rooms are defined by their recipes. Fert's floors include solid cover that stops parcels
- **Progression:** Familiar neighbourhood opens Lobby, followed by Restaurant, Pool, Spa, Residential floors and Penthouse. Each clear unlocks the next floor; unlocked floors remain replayable
- **Rooms:** doors remain entrances to optional special rooms. Stairs and elevators are automatic floor exits, not room doors

## Rooms

- **Enter:** every door sits within a visibly larger, coherent building or facade. Outdoor entrances share the door's world plane; distant parallax scenery does not replace their surroundings. Indoor doors use appropriate existing walls and trim. Use at a room's door plays the door's opening animation, if it has one, with play held, then shows the room as a full-screen still scene with a little animation, such as a bartender behind his bar. Play pauses until Chibi leaves, and leaving returns him to the door, shown closed. He leaves a skill gift room with its primary Continue button; ordinary rooms use Leave. Back, Esc and P open Pause or Menu according to the room's current pausability, without leaving it; Tab or an arrow focuses the room button, then Enter or Space presses it. A showing bark hides and holds its time while a door or room covers the stage
- **Gifts:** the stage doors offer seven Dash, seven Double jump and seven Speedy grants across the twenty-one stages, with their assignments in `src/data/zones.ts`. The grant is saved on entry, before any dialogue acknowledgement. A supportive placeholder animal presents one top-panel gift line, alongside an in-room skill-details card. That card is ordinary nonmodal room content and adds no separate pause or dim layer; entering the room still holds the outside stage until Chibi leaves
- **Per stage:** every stage has a skill gift room; repeated rooms make the skills easy to find and test
- **World:** the side-scrolling stages are the game world; rooms are the only other places, each reached through a door
