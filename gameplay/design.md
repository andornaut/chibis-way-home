# Game Design

The rulebook: every rule the game is built to. Tuning and release work are in [`README.md`](README.md), undecided questions are in [`ideas.md`](ideas.md), and agreed later scope is in [`next.md`](next.md).

`src/data/` holds every number and all player text. This doc states the rules, with numbers only where they define one.

## Pitch

A landscape platformer: Chibi travels back to the apartment he shared with his former best friend, Fert, a ferret, after Fert mails him far away. V1 follows one linear route.

## Setting

- **Characters:** Chibi is the male bird the player controls: a small, round, cream bird with tiny wings and feet, an orange bill and a dot eye. His full design is in the [art style guide](../art/style-guide.md#chibi). Animals talk; there are no humans.
- **Fert:** Chibi's male former best friend and apartment-mate: a champagne (cream-coloured) ferret, about twice Chibi's height, standing upright, with a tall plum stovepipe hat and a red bow tie. His full design is in the [art style guide](../art/style-guide.md#furt)
- **Story:** Chibi and Fert share an apartment in a big city. After a big fight, while Chibi sleeps, Fert packages him and mails him far away. Chibi knows Fert is responsible and wants to get home. The cause of the fight and the outcome of their friendship are undecided
- **World:** the modern day, mixing fantasy and reality
- **Zones:** a zone is one themed map destination containing playable stages, indoors, outdoors or a hybrid of both. V1 has six zones and twenty-one stages: three per outdoor zone and six Apartment floors. The V1 route and current stage availability are listed below

## Core loop

1. **A stage:** travel through one place, mostly left to right and bottom to top: platforming and talking with animals. The first five zones contain three stages each; Apartment contains six floor stages. V1 follows a linear route.
2. **Between stages:** completing a nonfinal stage shows an image-backed statistics interstitial and Continue, which enters the next stage directly. Completing a zone’s final stage shows the zone's outgoing exchange and statistics, then returns to the map and animates Chibi walking to the next zone, except for the final celebration. V1 continues along its linear route.
3. **Home:** Chibi returns to the shared apartment. The outcome of his friendship with Fert is undecided.

## Voice

- **Data:** every string a player sees lives in the text data files, with `{placeholders}` the game fills, never as a literal in code
- **Fit:** text never shrinks to fit. Reword it or grow its box
- **Labels:** one label for one action, everywhere. Player instructions name Move, Jump and Use with their matching inline control icons, rather than keyboard mappings; each icon and action label form one unit
- **Lengths:** text is Tiny5 drawn at 2×, about 6.5 base px a character on average; measure the width, don't count characters. A dialogue paragraph fits 3 lines of 504 px (about 72 characters a line). An answer fits 166 px (about 25 characters). A quip, with its speaker's name, fits 452 px (about 60 characters)
- **Spelling:** Canadian (colour, favourite, neighbour). No em dashes and no emojis
- **Style:** concise exchanges with distinct voices and brief jokes. Chibi stays determined to reclaim his home. Archie is earnest about his territory, Pebble is smug about flying, Trixi invents convenient rules, Bini is a distracted gamer, Kiwi is dry and observant, and Fert is theatrical about his failed delivery. Do not resolve the initial fight or their friendship
- **Terms:** the home page is the website's page at `/`; it is never shown inside an installed app. The start screen is the game's first screen, in a browser and in an installed app. Back to title always goes to the start screen, from any zone, the map or a card

## Rules

| Term               | Meaning                                                         |
| ------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Zone               | A themed map destination containing catalogued stages           |
| Stage              | The playable level, currently represented by `ZonePlan` in code |
| Section            | One continuous area within a stage, such as an apartment floor  |
| Encounter section  | A distinct enemy encounter segment within a stage               |
| Chunk              | A hand-designed unit used to assemble the randomized layout     |
| Opening and ending | The fixed caps around a stage's randomized middle               |
| Run                | One generated stage layout and its seed                         |
| Attempt            | One try to complete the stage within a run                      |

The V1 map has six themed destinations and twenty-one catalogued stages: three in each of the first five zones and six in Apartment. Completing the route’s implementation does not declare a V1 release.

### Screen and controls

- Landscape, 720×360 base resolution. Phone browser first, with on-screen touch controls; keyboard on desktop. In portrait on a touch screen, the game draws turned a quarter turn to fill the screen, so the player turns the phone
- **Keyboard:** ←/→ or A/D to move; Space, ↑ or W to jump; E, F or Enter to use; P or Esc to open or close the pause card
- **Mouse:** hold the left or right button over the unclaimed playfield to move left or right; holding both is neutral. A left click on a UI button performs its normal action without moving Chibi. A press claimed by UI stays consumed until release, including during a drag or hold. Mouse movement holds do not start a touch joystick or move hint. Release, blur and pause clear the corresponding movement state; touch controls remain separate
- **Overlay:** the move hint and the touch buttons show by default. Settings hides each set on its own, and hidden controls still work
- **Touch buttons** (right thumb): Use above, Jump below. Each is an open ring with its icon and no label, reads clearly as a button and stands out against any background, while hiding little of the play area. Every control stays inside the game's view across, with its drawn edge at least 8 px from the edge it is placed against: Pause 8 px in from the view's top and left, Jump 8 px in from the view's right and 20 px up from the screen's bottom, and Use 100 px above Jump with its ring 85 px in from the view's right. On a screen taller than 2:1, Jump, Use and the move hint sit in the spare space below the game. Each presses within 100 base px of its centre (a clear circle of 63 plus the move hint's radius of 37, so the hint never reaches into the clear circle), the two cut along the line midway between them so they never overlap; Jump's also reaches into the bottom-right corner of the screen. A button animates subtly when pressed
- **Touch movement:** a touch anywhere except on a button or other interactive element sets the neutral point where it lands. Dragging left or right from it past a small dead zone moves Chibi that way at full speed; back inside the dead zone, he stops. Releasing ends the movement
- **Move hint** (left thumb): a left and right marker that rests level with Jump, as far in from the view's left edge as Use is from its right edge. It is only a drawing and has no press area of its own. While a touch is moving him, it sits on the neutral point; on release it returns to rest.
- **Use and dash:** E, F or Enter and the touch Use button open an available room door when Chibi's drawn frame overlaps it, using the existing nearest-door rule. Otherwise a fresh press starts a short horizontal dash in the current movement direction, or his facing direction when there is no movement input. The direction stays committed through the dash. Dashing works on the ground and in the air, has a cooldown and allows one air dash until Chibi lands. Gravity and the jump curve remain unchanged. Terrain blocks the dash, and lethal contacts remain lethal; it grants no invulnerability. Tuning lives in `src/data/bird.ts`. Ordinary required routes never require dash or double jump; using them for shortcuts remains optional.

### Interface

- **Stage progress:** a thin, full-width bar stays fixed at the bottom of the gameplay HUD and has no input target. It shows the current stage fraction, including vertical route and area weighting, rather than the furthest position reached. Backtracking updates the current fraction; Retry resets progress to the current stage’s beginning

- **Consistent:** the same element looks and behaves the same on every screen: top bars, panels, close controls, buttons, headings
- **Disabled controls:** pressing an ordinary disabled control refuses activation with shared shake and red-label feedback. A locked stage card applies the refusal across its face, screenshot and both labels; keyboard selection skips locked stages. Reduced motion uses steady red feedback without shaking or flashing. Refusal never selects an answer or dismisses a card, and respects modal blocking, fresh-release and cancellation guards
- **Exact:** spacing sits on a fixed pixel grid: equal margins, aligned edges, and text clear of its box's edge by the same amount on both sides
- **Minimal:** show only what the player needs. No heading that repeats a tab's name, no label the picture already gives, and no decoration that competes with play
- **Options:** art mocks up visual options for the user to pick
- **Press areas:** apart from Jump and Use, a button's press area is the drawn button plus a quarter of its shorter side on each side, between 6 and 16 px, and at least 48×48. Where press areas overlap, the nearer button wins

### Grid

- **Chibi:** 2 tiles wide and 3 tall
- **Reaches:** stages are built to Chibi's reaches, measured in tiles from the top of the ground he leaves to the top of the ledge he lands on. A short press rises 3 tiles (clearing a 3-tile ledge with a little room); a full jump rises 6; an ordinary full running jump with flutter crosses a 17-tile gap at the same height, with the jump pressed at its best moment, coyote time included. A ledge at a reach is always makeable, and one tile beyond it never is
- **Tiles:** stages are built on a grid of 12 px tiles, so the screen is 60 columns by 30 rows. Every solid, ledge and door sits on whole tiles and spans whole tiles. Hazards follow the grid, except for the thickness of edge hazards
- **Travel distances:** generally author movement travel distances as whole multiples of the tile size defined in `src/data/screen.ts`, including dash and moving-object routes. Physics remains smooth and continuous: this does not quantize per-step displacement, speed, acceleration or gravity, or snap final positions to the grid. Collisions, support and endpoint constraints can stop movement between grid lines

### Chibi

- **Abilities:** new games start with no owned skills. Entering a gift room permanently grants Dash, Double jump or Speedy. Ownership and active selection are separate: exactly one eligible skill is selected, and only its effect applies. The first grant selects itself; later grants keep the current selection. Selecting another owned skill replaces the selection, never toggles the sole skill off. Revisiting an already owned grant changes nothing and does not stack it. Ordinary required routes remain completable without dash or double jump, while those skills can help with optional shortcuts, boss avoidance, secrets and dialogue access. There is no life counter or extra-life mechanic; later progression is listed in [`next.md`](next.md)
- **Double jump:** after leaving support, one fresh airborne Jump press can start an additional jump with half the first jump’s maximum rise. Its short-hop rise scales by the same ratio, with the existing release and hold controls. The ordinary first jump is unchanged. A grounded or coyote-time jump does not consume that extra jump. Actual supported landing restores it; holding Jump does not repeatedly trigger it
- **Flutter:** every fall flutters, after a jump or off a ledge: his fall is slow enough to use, and the player steers him in the air
- **Jump height:** every jump has one uninterrupted decelerating ascent, with no timed lift, upward reboost or pause at the apex while Jump is held. Releases within the first 120 ms produce the minimum hop of 40 px, about 3 tiles; releasing later blends continuously toward a higher apex, up to the full height of about 6.5 tiles, without a velocity snap, upward reboost or apex pause. Chibi flutters on the fall. A buffered jump whose button was already released is the low hop. Low ceilings are placed so a short jump clears the obstacle without touching the overhead red edge; a full hold hits that edge. Jump tuning lives in `src/data/bird.ts`
- **Jump timing:** a jump pressed just after he leaves a ledge still jumps (coyote time), and a jump pressed just before he lands jumps as he lands (jump buffering)
- **Movement:** he walks left and right and jumps. He accelerates and brakes smoothly on the ground and in the air. His speeds, acceleration, braking, jump, flutter and timing values are in `src/data/bird.ts`, tuned to the reaches under Grid

- **Skill controls:** eligible skills appear on the gameplay HUD. Owned or temporarily eligible skills stay visible; selection and resource state determine their appearance. Tapping an owned icon selects it; holding for the duration in `SKILL_GESTURE` opens details and pauses play. While Pause is open, pressing an eligible skill opens details instead of selecting it. Details replace the pause card, and closing them resumes gameplay instead of returning to Pause. Other modal and input owners remain respected
- **Skill states:** glyph and disc sizes and hit geometry stay fixed across enabled normal, cooldown and disabled states; enabling a skill never enlarges it. Colour, muting and the selection indicator distinguish states. Enabled normal means the selected powerup is drawn normally; enabled cooldown means it remains selected but is temporarily spent and muted. Both enabled states retain a steady dark selected ring. Disabled means another selected powerup supersedes it: the icon is muted without a selected ring. Selected Double jump stays normal while its extra jump is unspent, including on the ground, during coyote time and while Jump is held; spending it mutes the icon until actual support resets it. Selected Dash is muted during its active burst, actual cooldown or a spent airborne charge; being grounded or holding an input alone does not mute it. Selected Speedy stays normal as a passive effect
- **Speedy:** selecting Speedy multiplies normal horizontal running and airborne control speed by 1.2, without changing dash speed, jump height or acceleration. Repeated grants do not multiply it again

### Map

- **Availability:** all six V1 zones appear in route order, with their catalogued stages as listed under V1 route. Unbuilt zones and stages are visibly unavailable and cannot be entered. The park is a debug prototype outside the V1 map
- **Backtracking:** unlocked built stages remain available for replay. Replaying an earlier stage never removes later unlocks
- **Between stages:** clearing every stage shows an image-backed statistics interstitial with Continue at the bottom right. After a nonfinal stage, Continue enters the next stage directly without showing the map. A zone’s final stage first shows the outgoing zone exchange, then current-stage and cumulative zone statistics, before returning to the map and automatically animating Chibi walking along the existing route to the next zone. After the walk, the map waits for the player's selection; it does not enter that zone automatically. Completed stages and opened paths are marked. The final stage follows the celebration rule instead
- **Buttons:** Back to title is on the left; Continue is on the right and is the default keyboard action
- **Celebration:** only clearing Apartment building's sixth stage, Penthouse, shows Fert's outgoing exchange and the final statistics interstitial followed by the finish celebration, including on replay. Clearing the depot does not finish V1. Continue returns to the map; Restart asks first, as on the start screen
- **Checkpoints:** each stage begins at its own checkpoint, with unlimited retries. Walking left from any stage entrance returns to the map. The entrance exit stays reachable and has no blocking wall; an apartment stage returns through its first section's left edge
- **Forks:** V1 is linear and has no route forks or laps
- **Progression:** Parcel depot is unlocked from the start. Clearing a stage unlocks the next built stage in the route table. Clearing a zone’s final stage opens the next zone. Completed stages remain available for replay, and unbuilt content remains unavailable
- **Selection:** only manually pressing Continue on the map opens the selected zone's stage-selection modal. Up to three full-size side-by-side cards per page stay in catalogue order, including locked stages, which remain grey and disabled. Previous and Next controls change pages; the modal opens on the page containing the highlighted latest available stage. Keyboard navigation can focus unlocked stages across pages or activate enabled page controls. Each card shows an actual scene screenshot, the main stage name at the standard font size, and a smaller, lighter Stage {number} label using its catalogue ordinal at the top right. Names never shrink to fit. Stage scenery differs slightly while preserving the zone's palette and visual style. Persisted unlocks survive replays
- **Walk:** Chibi stands on a zone. Left and Right move through available zones, and Continue selects the zone he stands on

### Stages

- **Camera:** on the ground he sits about two thirds down the screen. In an assembled stage, the ground line follows the chunk he is in, blending across half a screen at each seam. The camera rises only when he climbs above the middle, and never shows below the lowest ground. Every catalogued stage guarantees some appropriate visible depth and retains plain chunks elsewhere. Farther decorative layers scroll more slowly, clipped to their selected chunk; Woodland tree layers cover horizontal challenges and terraces while vertical climbs remain plain. Apartment floors reveal depth through the fixed upper landing window, while ordinary rooms, the exit marker backing and doors keep opaque walls; exterior scenery does not appear through those walls. Ground props stay attached to the terrain
- **Challenge:** platforming, hazards and choices. Routes use ledges and cliffs, not ladders, with both short-press jumps beneath low ceilings and full jumps. There is no combat
- **Checkpoint:** the start of a stage is its only checkpoint. Falling or touching a hazard enters the death flow; Try again restarts the stage from its start, with unlimited retries and no permadeath. Progress is saved: the stages he has completed and where he stands on the map. Clearing a stage marks it completed and follows the nonfinal-stage interstitial, zone-end map travel or final-stage celebration flow; a stage left before its end is played again from its start
- **Crush:** a moving platform that pushes him sideways carries him to its leading edge. If it squeezes him into terrain or another solid, he is crushed and enters the death flow, as a hazard does. Layouts leave room beside every moving platform's path so he is never pinned on a required route
- **Difficulty:** per-zone bands and chunk-selection weights in `src/data/` set a demanding first-zone base and increase difficulty in every following zone. Blue cityscape selects grades 3 to 4, Industrial outskirts and Countryside grades 3 to 5, and Woodland, Home city and Apartment building grades 4 to 5. Increasing weights for harder chunks distinguish successive zones even when their bands match. Seeded variation remains inside each band
- **Doors:** optional room doors lead only into rooms; marked main-route stairs automatically link stage areas and are distinct from room doors. Use enters a door when any part of his drawn frame overlaps the door's drawn art; where he overlaps more than one, the door whose centre is nearest his wins
- **Edge hazards:** a common solid bright red strip, 4 px thick, can cover the top, bottom, left or right edge of terrain. Terrain hazards and floor traps use red contact edges. Pebble's falling white blocks are lethal projectiles. Animals have no permanent red contact outlines. There are no spikes. Touching any edge hazard enters the death flow. Actual red hazard contact shakes the world and Chibi through the main camera while the HUD stays fixed. Reduced motion replaces the shake with a static border cue. Animal contact, white blocks, falls and ordinary crushes do not trigger the red-contact cue
- **Encounters:** in the first five zones, stage one contains only the cinematic introduction and no normal hostile encounters; stage two has exactly two gameplay boss encounters, and stage three has exactly three. Apartment has no physical Fert or introduction in Lobby, Restaurant or Pool; Spa contains only the cinematic introduction, Residential floors has exactly two gameplay Fert encounters, and Penthouse has exactly three. Cinematic introductions are outside the gameplay encounter quota, and the actor starts leaving before normal play resumes. Only the scripted introduction is fixed. No normal antagonist is placed in a fixed opening or ending cap. Counts apply across the whole stage. Every second-stage opening has a protected first half-screen, then a mandatory noncombat obstacle, with its door and left map exit accessible. No physical antagonist or lethal attack enters that opening. Ordinary platforming separates encounters. Each normal hostile middle template appears at most once per stage; distinct templates in the same terrain family may still appear. Generated runs vary layout while preserving these quotas, and death retries retain the same run
- **Exit:** reaching a stage’s authored exit clears it; stages without one clear at the right end of their final area. Nonfinal stages show their image-backed statistics interstitials; a zone’s final stage shows its outgoing zone exchange and statistics before map travel, except for Fert's final exchange and statistics followed by celebration. An internal floor transition does not clear the stage
- **Going back:** walking all the way back to the stage's left edge leaves it without completing it: the screen fades and the map shows with him on that stage's zone. No completion dialogue, end-stage statistics or stage chooser opens. Completion progress is unchanged; a fresh stage entry starts a new generated run without resetting accumulated totals. Every stage starts him clear of that edge and has no wall in front of it
- **Kinds:** indoor, outdoor, or a hybrid with both
- **Length:** target about 60 seconds of active play for a successful ordinary required-route clear in the first five zones with no powerup active. Apartment floors retain their longer target of about 90 seconds. These are tuning targets, not hard timers; full human calibration remains incomplete. Dash, Double jump and Speedy are never required for the ordinary route. Recipe data owns each stage’s filled length range, including its fixed caps; the debug park keeps its own recipe
- **Layout:** every built and future stage, including the first tutorial and final stage, has a randomized layout assembled from hand-designed chunks: a fixed start chunk, a seeded run of chunks whose entry and exit heights match, then a fixed finish chunk. Each Apartment floor is one continuous stage area with its own fixed caps. The rules for chunks are under Chunks
- **Scale:** outdoors, the scenery is drawn at a quarter of indoor scale; Chibi, the tiles and the reaches stay the same everywhere
- **Shape:** areas scroll sideways and up, with width defined by their recipes. All twenty-one catalogued stages use a four-screen vertical capacity (120 rows, 1440 px). Blue connects lower routes and upper challenges; Industrial connects underground descents and ascents; Countryside connects terraces and an upper ferry; Woodland connects guarded lower routes, canopy lifts and red gates; Home city connects lower climbing yards and upper routes. Apartment connects its lower zigzag ascent to the retained upper climbing route, window and automatic exit. Indoors, to a small bird the ceilings are high: a door is 15 tiles tall, half the screen

- **Valleys:** every authored production valley has a physical floor with an explicit red lethal contact surface. The hazard is part of the terrain rather than an invisible fall threshold. Required crossings retain their safe banks, platforms and guarded middle ledges; off-world fall handling remains a safety fallback

### Runs and attempts

- **Continue:** reopening the app shows the start screen. Continue and Restart appear only after an actual catalogued stage has been entered. Manually choosing Continue shows the map with Chibi on his saved zone, without opening a stage chooser or entering a stage
- **Entry:** entering a stage from the map, replaying it or continuing through an inter-stage cutscene starts a new run
- **Pause:** pausing and resuming keeps the same run and position
- **Restart:** a full restart resets progress to its beginning state; the next actual stage entry starts a fresh run
- **Retry:** death ends the attempt; Try again restarts the same stage directly, without the map or stage chooser, as another attempt of the same run, preserving its seed and generated layout

### Stage design principles

- **Encounters:** build each arena around observing and responding to its antagonist’s special attack, with reachable refuges and exposed routes that make the choice meaningful
- **Horizontal crossings:** use compact 22-tile horizontal ferry bank gaps that require platform assistance for an ordinary single jump. Dash and double-jump shortcuts are allowed, but neither ability is required to board, ride or reach the far bank. Fit horizontal tracks to the banks, retaining shared mover speeds and endpoint pauses, 60-column chunk widths and stage-length budgets. Vertical mover routes remain unchanged. The [shared crossings](../src/data/chunks.ts) and [orbital crossings](../src/data/orbitHazardChunks.ts) own track distances and docking geometry.
- **Learning:** teach a demand clearly, vary it, then combine it with another demand. Introduce the response before requiring it under combined pressure
- **Moving platforms:** author and validate the complete approach, boarding, cycle, transfer and recovery sequence, not only jump reach. Validate and clearly present the available recovery options after missed boarding or transfers
- **Primary demand:** each challenge has a clear required action. Judge variety by the primary route, including required climbs rather than optional upper detours; decorative or bypassable obstacles do not establish a new demand
- **Readability:** show the hazard, approach and safe response before commitment. Provide observation positions and usable refuges so success does not depend solely on reacting after a short attack tell
- **Rhythm:** alternate pressure with recovery that permits observation and preparation for the next challenge. Preserve meaningful movement rather than filling stage duration with repeated waits
- **Variation:** vary required actions, not just chunk IDs. Repeated demanding sequences need variation in their required response; inserting a connector or changing a skin does not by itself provide that variation
- **Zone identity:** give each zone a recognizable combination and rhythm of traversal demands and antagonist interactions while using the shared layouts and physics

### Stage statistics and interstitials

Every clear, retry and zone-end presentation separates current-stage statistics from cumulative times:

| Group         | Field       | Meaning                                                                                                    |
| ------------- | ----------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Current stage | Attempts    | All attempts of this generated stage run, including the initial and just-finished attempt                  |
| Current stage | Last time   | Active play time in the attempt that just ended                                                            |
| Current stage | Best time   | Historically fastest successful attempt for this stage, excluding failed-attempt time                      |
| Totals        | Stage total | Active play time for this stage ID across all generated runs, retries and revisits since confirmed Restart |
| Totals        | Zone total  | Active play time across all stages and visits of this zone since confirmed Restart                         |
| Totals        | Run total   | Active play time across the whole playthrough since confirmed Restart                                      |

- **New runs:** a fresh generated run starts its attempt-local bookkeeping at zero and its attempt count at one. Death Retry preserves that run and starts the next attempt. Fresh map entry or replay generates a new seed and resets only generated-run statistics, not the displayed totals; historical best times remain
- **Presentation:** the full-page stage summary centres a 240 × 120 px actual stage preview above a 336 px-wide group of six statistics. The left column reads Last time, Best time and Attempts, with Attempts at the bottom; the right reads Stage total, Zone total and Run total. There is no divider, background fill, separate bottom Whole run row or death/completion-count display. Keep native fonts, spacing and the reserved footer with Continue at bottom right. Retry retains its compact centred modal, existing two three-row groups, divider and actions. Do not duplicate zone attempt or best-time columns. Outgoing dialogue retains its image-backed presentation and Skip
- **Statements:** retry and stage-transition statistics show one statement from the current stage’s zone antagonist, selected from the zone’s retry or clear line. This is a static stats statement, not a live world bark, so it does not require an on-screen world actor. There is no back-and-forth exchange, Skip or additional action: retain Back to title and Try again on retry, and the existing Continue on stage transitions. Present it once per actual stats entry, not again on rerender, modal resume or an action click; a new death creates a new retry entry. Death framing and its single animation remain unchanged
- **Whole run:** Stage total accumulates by stage ID, Zone total accumulates by zone, and Run total accumulates across the whole playthrough. Only confirmed Restart clears these three active-time aggregates; an initial store starts at zero. Fresh map entry, stage entry, retries, revisits and saved Continue preserve them. Paused time is excluded. Clear presentations snapshot completion after recording the cleared stage
- **Zone visits:** visit-local attempt and best-time bookkeeping remains separate from the cumulative displayed Zone total. Zone last-attempt time sums the latest finished attempt time from each visited stage; zone run-total time sums all active attempt time in those current-visit stage runs; zone attempt count sums their attempt counts. A zone best requires successful clears of every catalogued stage in the same visit and sums only those successful-attempt times. Compare it only with other complete visits. Partial visits retain their completed-stage count, set no new zone best and never combine historical stage bests into a fictitious full-zone result

### Death and retry

This is the approved implementation scope for the current change, including the death sequence, required card and persistent stage metrics.

- **Active time:** count only time spent playing the stage. The introductory approach and paused exchange do not count. Active play resumes when the antagonist starts its departure; ordinary pause and modal exclusions still apply. Pause, other paused conversations, menus, the death camera pan, sequence and card, floor-transition presentation, interstitials and map travel do not count. Floor transitions preserve the same run clock; its attempt and total times follow Stage statistics and interstitials
- **Card:** after the animation, hold the final pose beneath a required modal showing the zone and stage, current-attempt progress percentage, the three current-stage fields and the three aggregate totals. Back to title is the secondary button on the left; Try again is the primary button on the right. Keep the card compact and centred horizontally and vertically in the viewport. Keep the camera framing established before the death animation; opening the modal does not reframe Chibi or replay the animation. The card stays until a button is pressed; outside taps, back and Escape do not dismiss it
- **Progress:** a subtle, visible line 2 px high at the top uses the stage's theme. It tracks Chibi's current position during play and his death point on death, not the furthest point reached. In a multi-area stage it uses shared weighted current-position progress across the floor sections, including the death point; returning to an earlier section can lower it. Retrying starts the percentage at zero
- **Retry:** Try again restarts the current stage directly from its checkpoint in the same run, without the map or stage chooser. In a multi-area stage this is the first floor, not the current floor. Back to title opens the title screen. There is no permadeath
- **Sequence:** begin the visible camera pan immediately when the death trigger is handled, before the first death pose. Centre Chibi’s full authored sprite frame horizontally with its final bottom one tile (12 logical px) above the viewport bottom, preserving the authored padding rather than framing the silhouette and leaving visible context below him. Resolve the presentation’s landing support once at the post-recoil position. The distance-based pan uses the timing in `src/data/death.ts` and completes before the death animation starts; a zero-distance pan completes immediately. Keep this camera fixed through one recoil, fall, squash, bounce and seated animation, then open the modal once. A void death retains the full visible 96 px fall from above that final resting position. The world, player input, gameplay physics and active-time counting remain held throughout the pan and death presentation. Pan time never advances the death animation. On retry, title exit or shutdown, cancel any pending pan completion callback and contact-camera feedback before restoring the original camera; repeated restoration has no further effect
- **Skills:** ownership and the active selection persist across retries, stage changes and reopening, and reset on confirmed Restart. Capability fields in `src/data/bird.ts` describe mechanics, not saved ownership. Developer checked overrides temporarily add eligible skills; unchecked falls back to saved ownership without erasing it. A still-eligible selection remains selected; otherwise selection falls back to the first eligible skill. Developer selection is temporary, and previews and overrides do not write grants, selections or progress resets to the normal save
- **Storage:** historical best times use a separate stats save. Confirmed Restart clears Stage total, Zone total and Run total and starts fresh run statistics without wiping historical bests. Absent stage and zone cumulative ledgers initialize once at zero without changing other saved data, existing whole-playthrough totals or display labels; subsequent active play accumulates normally

### Chunks

- **Assembly:** every chunk, including fixed opening and ending caps, is exactly one screen wide: 720 px or 60 columns. Areas retain vertical scrolling; all catalogued stages use 120-row chunks; debug chunks retain their authored heights. - **Challenge variety:** the shared middle pools include required climbing routes, moving ledges, red ground strips and enemy yards. Connected climbs combine successive ordinary jumps with supported landings; controlled descents use readable lower passages and grounded recovery to limit overflight. Mandatory challenges include climbs, moving ledges, overhead and vertical red surfaces, and blocks comparable to Chibi’s size. - **Fairness:** routine isolated chunk checks use explicit required-route profiles, with slots empty and filled. Real-rule witnesses must connect from the actual entry to each declared required target and clear the actual exit; specified target intervals require grounded contact within their authored bounds. Optional surfaces are not required-route obligations. - **Seams:** a chunk's entry and exit are the ground rows at its left and right edges, as authored in its matching profile, including the deeper Industrial connectors. The seam surface is terrain and runs at least 4 tiles in from the edge, with no gap there, and nothing (no solid, ledge, hazard or moving platform) in the 6 rows above it. Initial unacquired placements and autonomous patrols respect this clearance; acquired pursuit and committed recovery may cross seams while respecting actual terrain and hazards
- **Shape:** all catalogued stages use 120-row chunks (1440 px); debug chunks retain their authored heights
- **Slots:** marked regions where the seed places an event or a door. A chunk must pass with every slot empty
- **Shared layouts:** every zone uses the same layout templates and object/archetype library. Theme configuration supplies terrain, structures, props, enemies, doors, triggers, backgrounds, parallax and recipes; it does not add zone-specific mechanics
- **Themes:** a theme sets the tileset, terrain looks and repeated backdrops for every chunk of that theme, so seams never show. A chunk can still place its own scenery. The park is an outdoor prototype theme
- **Background coverage:** every outdoor area has continuous interesting background coverage, including vertical challenges. ### Objects

Everything in a stage except its terrain is placed in data through archetypes, characters and instances.

- **Animals:** during normal gameplay, every animal in the main stage foreground, apart from Chibi, is an enemy and always lethal on actual body contact, including idle, tells, attacks and rest. Antagonist contact closely follows the drawn body footprint of the current animated pose; transparent frame space around wings or tails and gaps between drawn parts cannot kill on a visual near miss. Frame boxes may bound the contact check but do not make transparent space lethal. Chibi's movement and platform physics and the explicit red-hazard and projectile collider contracts are unchanged. Every antagonist regularly attempts direct charge or body contact and has a distinct recurring special attack; attack cadence and brief recovery windows are configured in data. Animals have no permanent red contact outlines. Archie, Pebble, Trixi, Bini, Kiwi and Fert remain the hostile foreground cast; decorative Woodland wildlife has no contact, interaction or gameplay reaction
- **Archetypes:** a behaviour: collision (none, solid, one-way ledge, hazard or Use), movement, reaction, interaction (such as talking on Use or a quip on approach) and default tuning
- **Attack sequencing:** each boss commits to one attack sequence at a time, retaining its identity through the required physical returns and completion boundary. The shared 2.5-second post-completion cooldown begins only after that sequence finishes; during it the boss can move and pursue rather than waiting in place. An aborted tell before commitment does not start this cooldown. Shared behaviour data owns its duration
- **Attack tells:** every aggressive charge or special action has a visible pre-action tell; the action begins only after the tell. Shared tells last between half a second and one second, with exact charge, special and growl durations in shared behaviour data. This includes swoops, drops, growls, pounces, throws and trap placement. A trap-placement tell is separate from the trap's warning and arming delay after placement. All six bosses play their small-hop or wing-lift anticipation pose once, then hold its final brace for the remainder of the tell. This is sprite animation, not a physical jump or a contact grace period. The renderer and lethal silhouette contact use the same authoritative animation clock and current frame. Only during an active attack tell, an outward red outline with an outer ink rim follows the current sprite frame’s actual alpha silhouette. The outline adds no circle, clearance gap or pulse and does not enlarge lethal contact. Outline widths and colours live in `src/data/bossWarning.ts`. Body contact stays lethal during tells and recovery
- **Behaviour patterns:** reusable patterns in the data choose behaviour primitives, their sequence or states, transition conditions, timing, animations and tuning. Animals and moving inanimate objects use the same composition approach. Code implements the primitives, condition evaluation and shared executor; character-specific cycles are composed in data rather than hardcoded in code
- **Characters:** who something is: one archetype plus tuning overrides, its art and animations, hit box, size, its dialogue and quip lines, and quirks. No character inherits from another. Props (platforms, hazards, doors and backdrops) are characters with art but no voice
- **Decoration:** friendly animals are allowed only as muted background flavour, never in the main stage foreground, or inside rooms reached through doors for shops and quests. Woodland deer stay fixed on actual ground while idling and grazing. Swallows make one autonomous flight arc from first actual on-screen visibility, then disappear without reversing or retriggering during that area visit. Wildlife uses ordinary world camera movement, never follows Chibi, and remains decorative rather than a physical actor. Pausing freezes the flight clock; retry or area entry resets it. Room animals remain in place
- **Enemy awareness:** all six pursuing bosses acquire Chibi when their current horizontal centres lie inside the same authored chunk in the current area, or when both physical body centres are inside the actual current viewport. Neither alternative requires line of sight or ground reach. Missing chunk metadata disables only the shared-chunk alternative; valid viewport awareness still applies. Rearming uses the same awareness alternatives with its existing settled and reachable guards. Attack-specific sight and exposure checks remain separate, and spotting barks still require an actual on-screen speaker at presentation
- **Enemy pursuit:** an acquired antagonist can chase across chunks in the same area, within six screen widths horizontally from its original encounter position for route planning. Brief loss of sight, a jump or temporary unreachability does not end pursuit. Pursuit ends when the current two-dimensional centre-to-centre distance from the boss to Chibi exceeds one screen width (60 tiles), producing an escape bark. Physical unreachability alone has no timed give-up; bounded planning failures can use frustration without declaring an unknown route unreachable. An escaped antagonist completes its committed physical recovery without starting another attack, then resumes normal behaviour near its stopping position without teleporting home. Ground bosses physically walk and jump across reachable ledges, blocks and gaps, with bounded route planning and safe landings rather than teleporting through cover. A supported jump route or a committed safe jump in flight counts as reachable; flying pursuit follows its target within physical clearance. Chase speeds derive from Chibi’s normal running speed so every boss can catch up and overtake him; closer attack engagement remains separately tuned. After escape, Pebble flies to nearby actual supported ground or a perch before resuming normal behaviour near his stopping position. If no safe reachable landing exists, he stays escaped with recovery pending, without attacking or teleporting. Charges, pounces, throws, traps, growls, vertical drops, swoops and physical perch returns use their authored local bounds, headings, speeds and timing from shared behaviour data. After escape and physical settling, connected-ground rearming is immediate when Chibi is inside the original boundary; jump-only routes require a bounded read-only proof from the same physical planner. Shared-chunk or current-viewport awareness applies to rearming; attack-specific current-screen and line-of-sight checks remain intact. Rearming checks occur on settled entry, shared-chunk or current-viewport awareness returning, support or goal-corridor geometry changes and existing patrol-leg boundaries. Valid proofs are reused and revalidated; movement alone does not repeatedly launch a search, and no awareness timer or new cooldown delays rearming. Unknown or exhausted planning work is not proof that Chibi is unreachable
- **Ground traversal preparation:** a verified grounded pursuit takeoff has a visible warning, with its tunable half-to-one-second duration owned by `GROUND_TRAVERSAL.prepSeconds` in `src/data/behaviours.ts`. The same once-played anticipation pose starts with actual preparation, including idle-recovery takeoffs, and holds its final brace until launch; cancellation restores the current behaviour pose. This is a physical wait, not a world freeze: moving support can carry the boss, support can be lost and hazards continue moving. After waiting, prove the takeoff again from its current position before jumping; an invalidated route does not launch. Escape cancels the pending takeoff. This preparation is separate from attack commitment and attack cooldown; ordinary pursuit walking continues without a takeoff warning
- **Idle recovery:** all six bosses share the watchdog configured in `src/data/behaviours.ts`. It arms only after actual acquisition and tracks horizontal physical displacement across graph changes and escape: vertical movement alone does not reset the clock. Two seconds without moving half a tile horizontally allows a local recovery search within four tiles, checking at most two candidate positions per attempt. It waits for a complete eligible physics tick rather than letting instant tell loops reset or starve that clock. Each boss makes independent seeded choices among safe lateral targets. Committed tells, recovery animations and airborne phases are protected. Recovery requires a verified supported ground position or perch and a safe swept route past hazards; if none is proved, the actor is not forced to move or teleported. Unknown geometry or exhausted work is not an unreachable verdict. Successful recovery resumes pursuit, and retry resets the watchdog
- **Instances:** one placement in a stage: a character, a position, size and phase, last-word overrides, its own lines or dialogue scene, a door's room, and flags for carried choices
- **Movement:** an algorithm (static, linear, ease, hop or wander), a direction, a distance, a speed, a pause at each end, and a random variance that rolls fresh on every attempt
- **Orbital hazards:** a red contact hazard can orbit an actual floating platform as the host moves horizontally or vertically. Its continuous circular path follows the host’s current position, and contact along the travelled arc is lethal. Boarding, riding and transferring require safe docks and timing around both the host’s travel and the orbit; the orbit does not replace the host’s collision or motion
- **Overrides:** flow one way: an instance overrides its character, which overrides its archetype. Everything resolves and is checked when the game loads
- **Reaction:** none, avoiding Chibi, or approaching him, within a range
- **Static art:** a behaviour state can use `animation: null` for static art, including a moving inanimate object. Motion and transitions still come from its reusable behaviour graph

### Boss encounter principles

Boss encounters are noncombat chase-and-escape platforming, not dedicated arenas or fights. Apply these criteria to each authored placement and its neighbouring route, not only to the shared behaviour graph.

- **Escape:** provide an ordinary route past the encounter to an open exit, with reachable supported refuges and room to react. Dash, Double jump and Speedy must not be required. Avoid trapping Chibi between lethal bodies, terrain and hazards without an ordinary response; cover must not permanently confine the boss either
- **Evidence:** qualify the actual placement, body dimensions, terrain and current behaviour together. Observe the warning, committed motion, accepted effects, contact, physical recovery and next attack boundary. A state transition, observed spawn or successful planner query alone is not a full encounter pass. Unknown or exhausted planning work is not proof of an impossible route. Automated response scripts state their reaction delay and limitations; human readability and enjoyment require playtesting
- **Flight:** provide overhead catch-up clearance and space for standoff swoops, avoiding stationary body blockades over the only route. Cover and landing supports must remain usable through the actual attack and return. Certify supported recovery before another attack; an airborne pause is not a perch or a completed landing
- **Identity:** give each boss one or two recognisable specials alongside ordinary pursuit and charges. Distinguish attacks through their actual movement, exposure, effects and ordinary responses, rather than only their names or animation tags
- **Readability:** retain the half-to-one-second tell with its visible pose and silhouette outline before commitment. Place encounters so the cue and a useful ordinary response are observable before contact; a tell does not make an already overlapping body safe. Rendered phase, animation frame, effects and lethal contact must agree. Do not credit an unseen cue or obscure it with another presentation
- **Recovery:** preserve one committed attack at a time and the 2.5-second cooldown after physical completion. Test a useful forward getaway through that cycle. Cooldown permits pursuit and body contact remains lethal, so cooldown duration alone does not certify safe passage
- **Spacing:** separate boss encounters have at least three intervening chunks with no encounter. Count actual placed chunks, including caps and across ordered areas of the same stage; reset the count for a newly loaded stage. A pursuing actor crossing a chunk seam remains the same encounter and is not stopped by this spacing rule
- **Traversal and variety:** place bosses on real support, with body-sized clearance and physical routes across relevant blocks, ledges and gaps. Vary supported spawn positions and encounter geometry through seeded authored choices, while Retry preserves its layout and seed. More entries with equivalent geometry do not establish more distinct challenges. Test ordinary player traversal separately from boss traversal, including approaches and departures across chunk seams

Other pause or modal owners still block play. The separate cinematic actor remains noncolliding and outside the encounter quota while it runs offscreen, and is removed offscreen or on scene shutdown. Both approach and departure use its maximum charge or dash speed from shared behaviour data. This cinematic sequence is outside the combat encounter quota; it does not make enemy contact safe during normal play. The first five zones introduce their antagonist in stage one; Apartment introduces Fert in Spa, its fourth floor. Earlier Apartment floors do not mark that introduction seen. The introduction plays once per zone per playthrough. Its seen state is shared across all stages in the zone and survives death, retries and stage replay; a new game resets it
- **Outgoing exchanges:** completing a zone's final stage shows its outgoing exchange over an actual image of the cleared stage's art, with Continue at the bottom right and Skip available. Completing or skipping the exchange proceeds to stage and cumulative zone statistics, then the map walk; Fert's final exchange and statistics proceed to the existing celebration instead
- **Restarts:** death retries and stage replays do not repeat a seen introduction; a new game resets the once-per-zone seen state
- **Skip:** Skip skips all remaining dialogue. If the conversation ends in a choice, Skip opens its modal without choosing an answer or bypassing the choice

### Quips

- **Barks:** each of the six antagonists has a single live spotting message with no Continue, Skip or other controls. Gameplay continues while it shows for up to five seconds, using `BARK_DURATION_SECONDS` in `src/data/dialogues.ts`. It appears once per area, object and attempt. Re-entering an area in the same attempt retains the seen state; retrying clears it. A bark starts only while its actual speaking world actor is rendered on screen in the current area and attempt. If that actor leaves view, dismiss the shown bark without requeuing it; keep unshown queued lines pending until their owner is visible. Only the panel background is translucent, using `BARK_BACKGROUND_ALPHA` in `src/data/dialogues.ts`; text and portraits remain opaque. Pausing the world freezes its timer, and modal cards appear above it. Paused multi-exchange conversations and their backgrounds remain unchanged, manually advanced and untimed
- **Panel taps:** tapping the visible dialogue rectangle invokes the same Continue action when Continue is present. Otherwise it dismisses only the current live bark, preserving queued and scheduled lines. A panel tap never skips an entire paused exchange; paused conversations remain untimed and manually advanced

### Fert

- **Appearances:** Fert enlists his friends as a favour. A friend or group is responsible for a themed zone and appears in person or through dialogue overlays; identities beyond Archie, Pebble, Trixi, Bini and Kiwi are undecided
- **Boss encounters:** the named antagonists use their existing contact attacks and specials. V1 has no dedicated boss arenas or combat
- **Calling card:** every trap Fert sets carries a badge of his stovepipe hat and bow tie
- **Ending:** Chibi returns home; the outcome of their friendship is undecided. There is no combat
- **Journey barks:** Fert has two authored live-bark schedule entries per zone, twelve across the journey, spaced after the introduction. Each entry still requires an actual visible Fert world actor at presentation; an entry without one remains unshown and never creates an actor or an offscreen voice. Each is a single five-second nonmodal message with no buttons, triggered by current weighted stage progress. Apartment messages occur on the restaurant and residential floors. Cinematics, modal scenes and active barks defer presentation. Only actual presentation marks a message heard; that state survives retries, area transitions and room visits within the generated run, while fresh stage entry resets it. Retry clears queued but unshown messages without marking them heard, leaving them eligible again when current progress reaches their threshold. Pausing the world freezes the message timer

### Choices

- **Effect:** a choice changes where Chibi goes, what he does and what he sees
- **Kinds:** dialogue choices always appear in a modal; V1 has no route forks
- **Reach:** choice effects on later zones or the ending remain undecided; see [`ideas.md`](ideas.md#open-questions)

### Opening story

New game and confirmed Restart show the storybook opening before the map; saved Continue bypasses it. Four manually advanced beats show Chibi and Fert’s friendship, their argument without explaining its cause, Fert packing the sleeping Chibi and the parcel travelling away, then Chibi waking far from home determined to return. Packing and postal transit are separate shots within the third beat, making five shots in total. Layered actor poses, gestures, parcel closure, travel and waking provide motion beyond pans over still pictures. The opening does not resolve their friendship.

Continue advances to the next beat immediately, without a mandatory animation wait; the final Continue opens the map. Skip ends the entire opening and opens the map. Completion or Skip marks the opening seen, separately from zone introductions. An interrupted unseen opening restarts at its first beat. Confirmed Restart clears that acknowledgement; cancelling Restart changes nothing. Opening playback and acknowledgement do not count as entering a playable stage.

### Start screen

- **Back:** back with no card open leaves the game, the same way as Exit, wherever Exit shows; elsewhere back is the browser's own
- **Boot:** the game opens in landscape. It always opens on the start screen, with Start alone until an actual catalogued stage has been entered; after that it offers Continue and Restart. It never opens automatically on the map or straight into a zone
- **Exit:** at the far top right in browser tabs, where it goes straight to the home page. It is hidden in every installed app
- **Restart:** on the start screen after an actual catalogued stage has been entered, and on the celebration. It asks first on a card: a short question, Restart, which confirms, and Cancel; back and Esc cancel. Cancelling changes neither progress nor action visibility. Confirming resets progress and the opening acknowledgement, then plays the opening before showing the map with him on the first zone and nothing completed. The start screen then shows Start alone until an actual catalogued stage is entered. Restart clears Stage total, Zone total and Run total while preserving historical bests and achievements. Confirmation is handled once; repeated input cannot repeat the reset
- **Settings:** at the top right, beside Exit in browser tabs and at the far right in installed apps. It opens the settings card
- **Start:** before an actual catalogued stage has been entered, Start plays any unseen opening, then opens the map with him on the first zone. Browsing the map, cancelling the stage chooser, returning to the title and reopening the app do not count as starting gameplay: Start remains the only play action, and Restart stays absent. Actual catalogued stage entry enables Continue, which opens the map where he stands without a stage chooser, and Restart beside it. There is one progress save

- **Updates:** waiting updates are offered manually only on Start, in a compact modal with a concise title, secondary Cancel on the left, primary Update now on the right and the ordinary close X. Only Update now accepts. Cancel, X, outside taps, Escape and back defer without reloading or discarding the waiting service worker. Dismissal lasts for the same Start visit and resets only on a new Start create, not on resume or settings close. Settings, restart and nested install cards suppress the offer while covering Start. There is no redundant body text

### Settings

- **Back:** back during play opens the pause card, and back with the card open closes it. Where the browser can't hand back to the game (Firefox for Android's installed app, and before the first tap or key in browsers without CloseWatcher), back is the browser's own
- **Back to title:** always goes to the start screen
- **Modals:** always centre every actual modal card horizontally and vertically in the viewport. Size each card's width to its content plus 16 px of shared padding on each side, with a minimum of half the full logical viewport width, then clamp it to the viewport's safe available width. Keep its height compact around the content with consistent padding. Preserve the standard text and control sizes; do not shrink them to fit a card. Every card over the game (the death card, the pause card, the settings card and its install steps, the restart question) has no title bar: its full title is centred across the full modal near the top, with symmetric clearance for the full close hit target and its gap on both sides. A closable card has a close X at its top-right corner. Keep the full standard font size: wrap the title or grow the card when needed, never shrink the text. Every actual modal card dims its background and blocks all underlying game, native, page and window controls, including keyboard input. Only the top modal receives input. Single-message barks are nonmodal and keep gameplay interactive; full-screen story and room pages are not modal cards. A tap anywhere outside a card that can close only closes it, as its close X or back would, the margins beside the game included; on a card with no close, a required choice, it does nothing at all. A press that drags does nothing
- **Open:** the pause button at the top left, shown only while a zone plays, P or Esc, or back. It also opens by itself when the frame turns, or stops turning, while a zone plays, and only then; Resume continues
- **Pause card:** a card titled Paused, over the dimmed game, which pauses play, with a close X. Its buttons, all one shape, stacked: Resume first, the primary in dark slate with white text, then Settings and Back to title
- **Settings card:** opened from Settings on the pause card or the start screen. Its rows: Move controls (the move hint) and Action buttons (Use and Jump), each a switch, on by default, then Install while the game can be installed, which opens the install steps. The build label sits at its foot. Its close X, P, Esc and back each return to whichever opened it
